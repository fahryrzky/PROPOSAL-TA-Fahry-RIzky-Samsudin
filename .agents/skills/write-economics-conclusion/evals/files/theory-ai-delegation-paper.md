# Theory paper materials: AI Delegation and Worker Initiative

> **Note:** This is a synthetic teaching and testing packet. All names, data, results, and citations are fictional.

The paper develops a two-period model in which a manager chooses delegation and a worker acquires costly task-specific information. An AI system supplies a public recommendation of known precision. The worker captures a share of project surplus through bargaining.

Under the baseline restrictions, delegation, worker information acquisition, and total surplus are hump-shaped in AI precision. Low-precision improvements reduce routine disagreement and increase delegation; beyond a threshold, the manager substitutes toward the recommendation and the worker's return to private information falls. Non-monotonicity disappears if workers capture no surplus or private information is costless. With strong bargaining, advanced AI can raise worker rents even as delegation falls. In a complementary-AI extension, recommendations increase the value of worker information and delegation rises monotonically.

The paper contains no data, calibration, welfare estimate, or policy experiment. The authors identify three model boundaries for future work: endogenous AI development, retraining that changes information costs, and organizational designs with multiple workers. They do not claim that firms should adopt or ban AI.
