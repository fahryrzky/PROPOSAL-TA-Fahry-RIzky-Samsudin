# Working paper materials: Remote-Work Adoption and Firm Output

> **Note:** This is a synthetic teaching and testing packet. All names, data, results, and citations are fictional.

The paper uses a 2017–2024 panel of 320 business-service firms. Firms voluntarily adopted formal remote-work policies. Firm and year fixed-effect estimates associate adoption with 6 percent higher output per employee. Output was already rising for two years before adoption, so the paper explicitly rejects a causal interpretation. Employment and wages rise after adoption; voluntary turnover falls by an imprecise 1.2 percentage points. A worker survey in 46 firms reports higher schedule control, but it does not test effort, monitoring, or office-cost channels.

The paper's contribution is a linked firm-worker panel and a description of the joint evolution of work arrangements and firm outcomes. The sample contains firms with at least 50 employees in business services. There is no quasi-experiment, office-cost data, or welfare analysis.

The current draft conclusion wrongly says that remote work causes a 12 percent productivity increase through office-cost savings and that governments should mandate it. The body instead concludes that adoption accompanies expansion and that richer designs are needed to separate policy effects from selection. It proposes collecting task-level adoption data and exploiting future policy discontinuities; these are explicitly identified as future work, not current results.
