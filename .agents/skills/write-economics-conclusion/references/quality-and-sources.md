# Conclusion Quality Gate and Source Map

## 1. Hard gates

The conclusion cannot pass if it contains:

- an invented or unsupported result, number, method, mechanism, limitation, citation, implication, or future direction;
- a wrong sign, unit, baseline, estimand, sample, period, or policy comparison;
- association presented as causation;
- a suggestive channel presented as established;
- a result introduced for the first time;
- a policy conclusion beyond the paper's evidence, welfare object, or external-validity scope;
- the primary takeaway omitted;
- unresolved placeholders or the wrong formal language.
- any unreported qualitative label that evaluates magnitude, cost, importance, robustness, efficiency, or policy attractiveness;
- any unsupported explanatory subordinate clause, appositive, modifier, denominator, baseline, comparison, or normalization even when the main clause is supported;
- any ranking of supportive evidence as a principal, main, or dominant mechanism;
- exploratory or secondary evidence given enough space to displace the first-order takeaway;
- a terminology substitution that changes the economic object or sample.
- a null result rewritten as proof of no effect, no harm, no erosion, or no change when the manuscript reports only no detectable or statistically significant change.
- a measured proxy silently rewritten as the broader latent outcome, or any closed-world paraphrase that upgrades scope, causality, mechanism strength, centrality, welfare content, or contribution.
- an identified treatment effect and a merely supportive mechanism merged into one causal proposition, or a latent channel used as the subject of an identified-effect sentence.
- `drove`, `explains`, `accounts for`, `operates through`, `mediates`, `because`, `by reducing`, or equivalent causal syntax applied to consistent-with, supportive, or suggestive mechanism evidence.
- an opening or closing field-wide prevalence/generalization claim such as `in many markets`, `often`, `widely`, or `common` without direct manuscript support.
- any evaluative adjective—including `modest`—without an explicit benchmark or source-authorized evaluation.
- a future-research proposal that invents a short horizon, cumulative effect, persistence concern, delayed response, outcome, mechanism, or threat not documented by the manuscript.
- a structural selection story that infers entrant ability, preparation, costs, or composition from another margin without an explicit model result.
- components called `offsetting` without opposite reported signs, or any decomposition direction filled in when the source says ambiguous or does not sign it separately.

These failures cannot be offset by fluent prose.

## 2. 100-point rubric

| Dimension | Points | Full-credit standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Factual fidelity and traceability | 30 | Every substantive statement, number, mechanism, limit, implication, and citation is supported |
| Genre function and module selection | 20 | Conclusion distills the paper and selects only modules that add interpretive value |
| Structure and logical flow | 15 | Findings precede or anchor interpretation; limits attach naturally; ending controls the correct takeaway |
| Economic and identification language | 10 | Causal, model, mechanism, welfare, policy, and external-validity wording is calibrated |
| Length and information density | 10 | Length reflects interpretive workload, not repetition |
| Natural academic English | 10 | Clear, confident but bounded, non-template synthesis |
| Mode and delivery experience | 5 | Correctly drafts, rewrites, audits, or fact-checks without unauthorized edits |

A score above 95 requires an accurate central takeaway and no generic filler masquerading as mechanism, limitation, policy, or future research.

## 3. Final checklist

- Does the opening re-anchor the actual paper and give the distilled answer?
- Are only first-order findings and decision-relevant quantities retained?
- Is every mechanism labeled at its true evidence level?
- Is each limitation specific and attached to the claim it changes?
- Does any robustness language avoid claiming all threats are gone?
- Does future research grow from a documented boundary?
- Are implications explicit but proportionate to scope and assumptions?
- Are citations, if any, verified and doing necessary interpretive work?
- Does the conclusion synthesize rather than copy the abstract, introduction, or tables?
- Is the last sentence the right accurate departure impression?
- Has the output passed both directions of evidence review?
- Does every restatement of a null preserve its detection/significance qualifier, measured proxy, specification, and scope?

## 4. Normative and calibration layers

- **Guide-derived norm:** distill rather than repeat; explain supported mechanisms; admit specific limits honestly; derive concrete future directions; state broad implications with restraint; cross-check the introduction; remain short and focused.
- **Sample calibration:** ten recent AER/QJE/JPE/Econometrica conclusions show that only the distilled answer is universal. Mechanism, limitations, future work, policy, literature links, and historical framing are selected modules. Limits often sit next to the claims they qualify, and long conclusions earn their length through interpretation rather than result repetition.

Sample frequencies and extremes do not replace the guide. In particular, one very long conclusion does not invalidate “short and sweet.”

## 5. Development provenance

Primary development sources:

- A bilingual conclusion-writing guide authored by the skill author, synthesizing the works credited in the repository NOTICE file;
- a bilingual compilation of conclusions from ten published economics papers, used only as structural calibration;
- machine-readable conclusion data extracted by the author from the same ten papers;
- Anthropic's public skill-authoring documentation (skill-creator).

The original guide cites Cochrane, McCloskey, and selected paper conclusions. Preserve the knowledge but do not invent or automatically add citations to a user's conclusion.
