## 1. Purpose and governing test

A conclusion is the clean snapshot the reader carries away. It should state what the paper established, why that matters, and where the conclusion stops. It is not another abstract, a shortened introduction, or a replay of the results section.

The guide's “short and sweet” principle is a discipline of selection. The operational deletion test is: if removing a sentence does not change the reader's understanding of the paper's central claim or its essential boundary, remove or compress it.

## 2. Distillation

### Principle

Begin by re-anchoring the research question, setting, or framework and giving the distilled answer. Select only the findings required to support that answer.

### Rationale

The evidence and derivations already appeared in the body. The conclusion adds value by organizing them into a judgment, not by recounting the path table by table.

### Quantities

One or two quantities may be valuable when they preserve economic magnitude, a welfare comparison, or a policy threshold. “Few numbers” does not mean “no numbers.” Verify every number and avoid coefficient inventories.

### Synthesis

Use contrasts, null results, or heterogeneous effects when they change the overall interpretation. Do not let a striking secondary result displace the first-order conclusion.

## 3. Mechanisms and economic intuition

### Principle

A conclusion can explain why the pattern arises and when it should travel to other settings. Specific mechanism or economic intuition often turns a statistical result into understanding.

### Evidence condition

Only include a mechanism when the body supports it. Distinguish:

- directly identified or tested channels;
- mechanisms internal to a model and its assumptions;
- evidence ruling out alternatives;
- patterns consistent with a channel;
- suggestive evidence;
- speculation.

Do not write `the mechanism is` when the paper only offers suggestive evidence. Use calibrated language or omit the channel.

### Boundary function

Mechanisms often pair naturally with heterogeneity or a counterexample. Explaining when a channel does not operate can be as important as naming when it does.

## 4. Limitations and boundaries

### Principle

Specific limits increase credibility by calibrating the claim. Generic disclaimers do not.

A useful limitation states:

1. the exact threat, sample boundary, institutional feature, period, measurement limit, model assumption, or missing margin;
2. which claim it changes and in what way;
3. what the paper did in response;
4. what remains unresolved.

Robustness and sensitivity analyses can narrow a concern; they rarely prove that every threat is gone. The target voice is vulnerable confidence: clear about what the evidence supports and honest about what it cannot rule out.

### Important exception

Do not force a limitation paragraph into every conclusion. If the supplied manuscript does not identify a reliable limit, do not invent one from generic disciplinary anxieties. Ask when essential or state only the supported scope.

Place a boundary near the claim it qualifies rather than collecting disclaimers at the end.

## 5. Future research

### Principle

Future research should grow from a boundary the paper has actually revealed.

Good directions identify:

- an unresolved mechanism;
- an untested population, institution, or period;
- a data or measurement gap;
- a missing equilibrium, scale, or implementation margin;
- a model assumption whose relaxation matters;
- a new question directly opened by the result.

Avoid `more research is needed`, generic wish lists, and projects invented by the agent. A conclusion need not contain future work if no concrete direction is evidenced or useful.

## 6. Implications

### Principle

Move from the findings to theory, policy, welfare, history, institutions, or real-world understanding, but match the verb and scope to the evidence.

Possible calibrated forms include `shows`, `suggests`, `points to`, `is consistent with`, `can inform`, and `provides limited support for`. These are not interchangeable decorations; choose based on evidence strength.

Avoid `proves`, `requires`, `must`, or universal policy conclusions unless the manuscript genuinely establishes the necessary premises. Do not turn a local or partial-equilibrium estimate into a general welfare recommendation.

Implications are conditional modules. A precise boundary can be a better ending than a weak policy paragraph.

## 7. Consistency and citations

After drafting, compare the conclusion with:

- the title's promise;
- the abstract's central result;
- the introduction's question and contribution claims;
- the main results and mechanism sections;
- the manuscript's limitations and policy analysis.

If the introduction overpromises, write to the delivered body and flag the mismatch rather than reproducing it.

Unlike an abstract, a conclusion may contain citations when they perform real work: positioning a result, supporting a policy or institutional fact, interpreting external validity, or connecting to theory. Use only citations present in or verified for the manuscript. Do not turn the conclusion into a literature review.

## 8. Paper-type adaptations

### Simple empirical paper

Re-anchor, synthesize the main effect and one boundary, then close with a restrained implication or future question. A short conclusion can be complete.

### Multi-result causal paper

Build one hierarchy rather than a list. Explain how the results combine, use mechanism and heterogeneity only when central, and condition implications on the studied design and population.

### Structural or policy paper

Explain the model's key economic tradeoff, the principal counterfactual, uncertainty or model dependence, and the conditions under which the policy interpretation changes.

### Theory paper

Organize around broad lessons, mechanisms, comparative cases, parameter conditions, and distributional implications. Future work may identify model margins not endogenized. Do not mimic an empirical limitations template.

### Historical paper

A return to the historical frame can be effective when it deepens the interpretation. Connect to the present only through supported continuity or contrast, not analogy alone.

### Measurement paper

Clarify what the measure reveals, why heterogeneity or sorting matters, and what use or policy reallocation follows only under supported assumptions.

## 9. Anti-patterns and repairs

| Failure | Why it fails | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract repeated in new words | Adds no synthesis | State the distilled answer and explain how findings combine |
| Table-by-table recap | Preserves chronology instead of meaning | Select the main line and boundary-changing evidence |
| Mandatory five-module checklist | Induces invented mechanisms, limits, and policy | Select only modules licensed by evidence and purpose |
| Generic limitation | Does not calibrate a claim | Name the exact boundary and its consequence |
| Robustness as certainty | Overstates what sensitivity checks prove | State what the check addresses and what remains |
| Established mechanism from suggestive evidence | Misrepresents support | Qualify explicitly or omit |
| “Future research is needed” | Provides no actionable intellectual next step | Tie a concrete question to a demonstrated boundary |
| Policy manifesto | Exceeds design and external validity | Use a conditional, evidence-matched implication |
| New citation or fact | Introduces unverifiable material | Use verified manuscript sources only |
| Long because it repeats | Consumes space without interpretive work | Expand only for mechanism, boundary, policy, or model explanation |
| Copied introduction language | Repeats promises rather than delivered evidence | Recompose from results and the evidence ledger |

## 10. Rewrite and audit modes

When rewriting, preserve supported substantive content but remove repetition and unsupported extension. Do not “improve” confidence by dropping qualifications.

When auditing, separate:

- factual additions and contradictions;
- missing central takeaway;
- module selection;
- mechanism and causal calibration;
- boundary specificity;
- implication and future-work support;
- order, length, and repetition;
- natural academic English.

An audit request does not authorize a silent full rewrite.
