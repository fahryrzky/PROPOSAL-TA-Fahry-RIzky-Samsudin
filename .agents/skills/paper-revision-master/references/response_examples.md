# Response Examples for Different Comment Types

This file provides model responses for different types of referee comments. Use these as templates for crafting your own responses.

---

## CLARIFICATION Responses

### Example 1: Adding Missing Information

**Comment:** "The author should more clearly explain the identification strategy."

**Response:** 
We thank the reviewer for this suggestion. We have added a detailed explanation of our identification strategy to Section 3 (page 8, lines 15-25). Specifically, we now explicitly discuss how our difference-in-differences design isolates the causal effect of [X] on [Y], including the key identifying assumptions and why we believe they are satisfied in our context.

### Example 2: Strengthening Literature Review

**Comment:** "The literature review is incomplete. The author should discuss the recent work by Smith et al. (2023) and Johnson (2022)."

**Response:**
We appreciate this guidance and have now incorporated discussion of both Smith et al. (2023) and Johnson (2022) in our literature review (page 4, lines 10-18). We discuss how Smith et al.'s finding of [X] complements our results, and we address Johnson's methodology in relation to our approach.

### Example 3: Clarifying Ambiguous Claims

**Comment:** "On page 12, the author claims that 'the effect is substantial' without providing context for what 'substantial' means."

**Response:**
Thank you for pointing this out. We have revised this sentence to provide specific context: "The effect is economically substantial—a one standard deviation increase in [X] is associated with a 15% increase in [Y], which translates to approximately $X in annual terms for the average firm in our sample." (page 12, lines 5-8)

## NEW ANALYSIS Responses

### Example 1: Adding Robustness Tests

**Comment:** "I suggest conducting robustness tests using alternative model specifications."

**Response:**
We agree that robustness checks strengthen the paper. We have added a new robustness section (Section 5.3, pages 18-20) where we report results using:
1. Alternative dependent variable: [Y2] instead of [Y1]
2. Alternative sample: excluding observations with [characteristic]
3. Alternative estimation: [different method]

All robustness results are consistent with our main findings. See Table 5 for the detailed results.

### Example 2: Subsample Analysis

**Comment:** "The analysis should be conducted separately for [subgroup A] and [subgroup B]."

**Response:**
Following the reviewer's suggestion, we have estimated our model separately for [subgroup A] and [subgroup B]. The results (Table 6, columns 1-2) show that the effect is indeed stronger for [subgroup A] (coefficient = X.XX, p < 0.01) compared to [subgroup B] (coefficient = X.XX, p < 0.05). This heterogeneity aligns with our theoretical prediction that [mechanism]. We discuss these differences on page 22, lines 3-10.

### Example 3: Placebo Tests

**Comment:** "I recommend a placebo test to rule out the possibility that the results are driven by [alternative mechanism]."

**Response:**
We thank the reviewer for this excellent suggestion. We have conducted a placebo test using [falsification outcome/pretreatment period/different population] and find no statistically significant effect (coefficient = X.XX, p = 0.XX). This result supports our identification strategy by showing that [interpretation]. See Table 7, column 3, and the discussion on page 24, lines 1-8.

## DISAGREE Responses (Require User Approval)

### Example 1: Beyond Scope

**Comment:** "The author should collect data from [additional country/time period] to test whether the results generalize."

**Response:**
We appreciate the reviewer's suggestion to extend our analysis to [additional context]. While we agree that understanding generalizability is important, collecting data from [additional country/time period] would require a substantial new data collection effort that extends beyond the scope of the current paper. Our contribution is to establish the effect of [X] on [Y] in the [current context], and we believe this is a valuable first step. We have added a discussion of this limitation in the Conclusion (page 28, lines 12-15) and note that future research could examine whether our findings extend to other contexts.

### Example 2: Methodological Disagreement

**Comment:** "The author should use [Method B] instead of [Method A]."

**Response:**
We thank the reviewer for raising this methodological point. While [Method B] has merit in certain contexts, we believe [Method A] is more appropriate for our setting because [specific reason: e.g., our data structure, established precedent in the literature, specific assumptions that are more reasonable]. We have added a footnote (footnote 8) explaining this choice and citing prior work that validates [Method A] in similar contexts. That said, we acknowledge that different methodological choices could yield different insights, and we note this as a limitation.

### Example 3: Infeasible Request

**Comment:** "The author should conduct a randomized experiment to establish causality more convincingly."

**Response:**
We agree that a randomized experiment would provide strong causal evidence. However, conducting such an experiment is infeasible in our context because [specific reason: practical constraints, ethical considerations, cost, etc.]. Instead, we have strengthened our causal identification by [what you actually did: added robustness tests, placebo tests, addressed endogeneity concerns, etc.]. We believe this approach provides compelling evidence given the constraints we face, and we note this as a limitation (page 28, lines 5-8).

## Tips for Effective Responses

1. **Always start with gratitude:** "We thank the reviewer..." or "We appreciate this suggestion..."

2. **Be specific:** Cite exact page numbers, line numbers, and table numbers where changes were made.

3. **For NEW ANALYSIS:** Briefly explain what you did, then point to the specific location where results are reported and discussed.

4. **For CLARIFICATION:** Quote the specific text you added or revised.

5. **For DISAGREE:** Be diplomatic. Acknowledge the merit of the suggestion, then clearly explain why it was not implemented. When possible, offer an alternative.

6. **Never be defensive:** Assume the reviewer is trying to help improve the paper, even if the comment seems misguided.

7. **Keep it concise:** Most responses should be 3-5 sentences. Don't repeat yourself.

8. **Group related comments:** If multiple reviewers raised the same issue, note that you addressed it in response to multiple comments.

## Common Phrases

**Acknowledging comments:**
- "We thank the reviewer for this insightful comment."
- "We appreciate the reviewer's careful reading and helpful suggestion."
- "This is an excellent point that has helped us improve the paper."

**Describing changes:**
- "We have revised [section] to [action]."
- "We have added [content] to [location]."
- "Following the reviewer's suggestion, we have [action]."

**Pointing to evidence:**
- "See Table X, column Y, for the detailed results."
- "This change appears on page X, lines Y-Z."
- "We discuss this on page X of the revised manuscript."

**Polite disagreement:**
- "While we appreciate this suggestion, we believe..."
- "We have considered this approach but decided against it because..."
- "This is an interesting idea, but it extends beyond the scope of our current work."

**Offering alternatives:**
- "Instead, we have [alternative action]."
- "As an alternative, we have added [content] to address the underlying concern."
- "We hope to explore this question in future research."
