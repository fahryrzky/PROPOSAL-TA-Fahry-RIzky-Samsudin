# Rhetoric and organization diagnostics

Run each applicable criterion as a separate pass under [diagnostic-protocols.md](diagnostic-protocols.md). Do not combine the criteria into one generic rhetoric judgment.

## CRGP

**Applicability:** Introduction and, when useful, compact rhetorical sequences in an Abstract. Do not force CRGP labels onto Methods, Results, or unrelated sections.

**Input window:** the complete Introduction, paragraph identifiers, and paragraph-function summaries. Inspect raw paragraphs when assigning moves.

**Intermediate representation:** label each relevant paragraph or sentence as `[C]` Context, `[R]` Review, `[G]` Gap, `[P]` Purpose, or a justified combination. Record the sequence and the evidence for each label.

**Decision test:**

1. Does Context establish the problem area without irrelevant breadth?
2. Does Review synthesize what is known in a way that leads to Gap?
3. Is Gap specific, defensible, and addressable by the study?
4. Does Purpose answer the stated Gap?
5. Are moves missing, duplicated, reversed, or disconnected?

**Repair boundary:** reorder or condense only when the move sequence is demonstrably broken. Do not invent a gap, contribution, or literature claim. Unsupported gap claims become `QUERY`.

**Recheck:** relabel the complete Introduction and verify that Review supports Gap and Purpose answers Gap after revision.

## L1--L3 scope progression

**Applicability:** Introduction and Literature Review.

**Input window:** all paragraph-function summaries for the relevant section plus the raw paragraphs at every scope transition.

**Intermediate representation:** assign `[L1]` general area, `[L2]` sub-area, and `[L3]` key topic to each unit. Record the sequence.

**Decision test:** flag an unexplained jump to technical detail, a return to broad background after the key topic is established, repeated L1--L3 oscillation, or a concept used before its contextual level is available.

**Repair boundary:** reorder, narrow, or add only source-supported orientation. Do not create background facts.

**Recheck:** read only the scope labels and then the transition passages; both must show a defensible progression.

## Section and paragraph function

**Applicability:** every in-scope section and paragraph.

**Input window:** the full section for paragraph analysis; the full manuscript outline for section analysis.

**Intermediate representation:** write one factual function sentence for each unit, plus what the reader knows before it and what it enables next.

**Decision test:** identify units with no function, two independent functions, duplicated functions, unsupported detours, misplaced material, or a missing function required by the surrounding argument.

**Repair boundary:** split, merge, move, delete, or bridge only when the function map demonstrates a concrete cost. Major moves and substantive additions require confirmation.

**Recheck:** rebuild the reverse outline and confirm that every unit has a distinct, necessary role.

## Gap--Purpose alignment

**Applicability:** Introduction, Abstract, and Conclusion when they state the research gap or purpose.

**Input window:** the Gap passage, Purpose passage, relevant Review synthesis, and restatements of purpose elsewhere.

**Intermediate representation:** decompose Gap into `object`, `missing knowledge/capability`, and `consequence`; decompose Purpose into `object`, `action`, and `intended resolution`.

**Decision test:** compare components one by one. Flag a purpose that addresses a different object, overpromises resolution, leaves part of the Gap unanswered, or introduces a task not motivated by the Gap.

**Repair boundary:** align wording to the study as written. Do not enlarge the study or manufacture a contribution.

**Recheck:** every Purpose component must map to a Gap component and to an actual method or result.

## Cross-section research-question alignment

**Applicability:** multi-section manuscripts.

**Input window:** Abstract, stated questions/hypotheses in Introduction, Methods overview, principal Results, Discussion interpretation, and Conclusion.

**Intermediate representation:** build a table linking each research question to method, result, interpretation, and conclusion.

**Decision test:** flag unanswered promises, results without a stated question, discussion claims without results, conclusions that add new findings, and inconsistent question or contribution wording.

**Repair boundary:** revise representation and organization, not underlying research. Missing research content becomes `QUERY`.

**Recheck:** rebuild the table after revision and confirm one defensible path for every question.

## Literature-review organization and synthesis

**Applicability:** Literature Review and literature-heavy Introduction passages.

**Input window:** a complete thematic block or section, including citations and its concluding synthesis.

**Intermediate representation:** assign each paragraph an organizing basis: question, position, method, evidence, debate, chronology, or author list. Record source attribution and the block's synthesis claim.

**Decision test:** flag author-by-author catalogues that obscure the research problem, comparisons without a common basis, missing synthesis, abrupt theme changes, or attribution that makes a source claim appear to be the present author's claim.

**Repair boundary:** reorganize only from claims and citations already present. Do not infer literature consensus or source findings that are not available.

**Recheck:** read paragraph topics and synthesis statements; the block must develop one explicit question or comparison and preserve attribution.
