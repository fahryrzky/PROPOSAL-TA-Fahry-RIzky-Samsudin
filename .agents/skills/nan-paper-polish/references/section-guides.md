# Section-specific revision guides

Read only the sections relevant to the request.

## Abstract

- Build a compact Context-Gap-Purpose-Method-Result-Contribution sequence appropriate to the field.
- Minimize generic background and literature review.
- State purpose, method, and principal result with concrete subjects and verbs.
- Report substantive findings rather than `results are discussed`.
- Preserve numbers, direction, uncertainty, and study scope.
- Calibrate causal, mechanistic, practical, and generalization claims.
- Check every claim against the main text.

## Introduction

- Mark `[C][R][G][P]` and `[L1][L2][L3]`.
- Establish significance with consequences or evidence, not unsupported adjectives.
- Organize Review around the problem that leads to Gap.
- Avoid author-list summaries.
- Make Gap specific, defensible, and solvable by the present study.
- Align every stated purpose, question, or hypothesis with Gap.
- Use past tense for specific completed studies, present perfect for research activity extending to the present, and present tense for current knowledge when appropriate.
- Use parallel syntax for objectives and questions.

## Literature Review

- Organize by question, position, method, evidence, or debate.
- Maintain stable technical terms and topic chains within each theme.
- Distinguish source reports from the present author's synthesis.
- Use author prominence when scholarly agency or disagreement matters.
- Use information prominence when knowledge state or topic continuity matters.
- Calibrate consensus, disagreement, gaps, and criticism.
- Conclude thematic blocks with an accurate synthesis or bridge.

## Methods

- Preserve all reproducibility details.
- Present steps, variables, materials, and conditions in parallel forms.
- Put procedural actions in clear verbs.
- Use passive voice when process or material is the established topic; use active voice when agency or analytical choice matters.
- Unpack ambiguous noun strings without changing established terms.
- Preserve approximations, assumptions, software versions, parameters, exclusions, and stopping rules.
- Do not remove repeated information when it is necessary for reproducibility.

## Results

- Give each paragraph a result topic before supporting detail.
- Move from research question or analysis to result, then evidence.
- Use variables, groups, models, or findings as visible topics.
- Present peer results in parallel structures.
- Use precise summary nouns for tables, figures, and patterns.
- Report values, direction, intervals, and uncertainty exactly.
- Distinguish `did not detect` from `there was no effect`.
- Avoid introducing untested explanations as findings.

## Discussion

- Build a continuous sequence: result, interpretation, relation to literature, implication, limitation.
- Distinguish direct result from interpretation and mechanism.
- Calibrate causal claims, alternatives, and generalization.
- Use `this finding`, `this pattern`, or `this discrepancy` only when the antecedent is exact.
- Compare with prior studies without changing attribution.
- Keep limitations close to the claims they constrain.
- Avoid hedge stacking while preserving uncertainty.

## Conclusion

- Return to Gap and Purpose and state what was actually resolved.
- Do not merely repeat the Abstract.
- Preserve the evidential strength used in Results and Discussion.
- Scope practical recommendations and future implications.
- Do not add a new result, mechanism, citation, or limitation.

## Titles, headings, tables, and figures

- Keep terminology consistent with the text.
- Verify numbering and every cross-reference after structural changes.
- Do not alter values in captions or tables during language editing.
- Ensure headings reflect section function and hierarchy.
- Preserve abbreviations and units consistently.
