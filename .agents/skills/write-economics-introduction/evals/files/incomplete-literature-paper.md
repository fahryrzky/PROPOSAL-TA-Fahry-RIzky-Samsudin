# Synthetic manuscript packet: workplace scheduling and retention

The completed body studies the association between advance schedule notice and six-month employee retention in a panel of 260 retail establishments from 2018–2024. Establishments posting schedules at least 14 days ahead have 3.5 percentage points higher six-month retention than establishments posting schedules less than 7 days ahead, conditional on establishment and month fixed effects. The result is associational. A pre-trend plot shows that establishments which later adopt advance scheduling already had improving retention. The paper therefore emphasizes selection and does not claim a causal effect.

Manager interviews suggest that predictability may help workers coordinate care responsibilities, but interviews were not systematically sampled and do not establish a mechanism. The body contains no policy evaluation.
