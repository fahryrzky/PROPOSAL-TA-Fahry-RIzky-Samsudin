# Synthetic manuscript packet: AI screening and delegated search

This is an original development case. All references are fictional and defined here.

## Question and environment

The paper asks how an imperfect but scalable AI screening technology changes delegation inside firms when workers search for projects and managers decide which projects receive costly review. There are no data, estimates, or empirical tests.

Workers differ in search ability. Projects differ in quality, which is initially unknown. Managers have limited review time and can either review a project themselves or rely on an AI signal. The AI signal has fixed precision and can be applied at scale, but false positives consume managerial attention when forwarded for review. Contracts condition on accepted projects, not on search effort or rejected ideas.

## Main propositions

Without AI, higher-ability workers receive more delegation because they generate better projects and waste less review capacity. Introducing AI has a non-monotonic effect on delegation as signal precision rises. At low precision, AI creates many false positives and centralizes review. At intermediate precision, it substitutes for initial managerial screening and expands delegation. At very high precision, the firm routes most projects through AI and reduces workers' discretion over which projects advance; delegation falls again under the paper's definition.

The non-monotonic result requires scarce managerial review capacity and false positives that consume that capacity. If review is costless or false positives can be discarded without managerial time, delegation is weakly increasing in AI precision. Total firm surplus increases with precision in the baseline model, but worker pay can fall for intermediate-ability workers because the technology changes the return to private search information.

An extension makes AI complementary to worker knowledge: signal precision increases with the worker's privately chosen preparation. In that extension, delegation can become monotone because stronger incentives to prepare offset the review-capacity channel. The paper does not claim that complementary AI is empirically more common.

## Verified literature packet

**Faber (2017), “Delegation under Scarce Review.”** Studies delegation when managers have limited review time but no automated screening.

**Imai and Costa (2021), “Algorithms as Noisy Gatekeepers.”** Studies a one-agent screening model with exogenous project arrival and no worker search or contracting.

**Dube and Lind (2023), “Automation and the Allocation of Attention.”** Studies scalable automation and managerial attention in a team model, but not endogenous delegation over project advancement.

Supported positioning: the paper connects theories of delegation under scarce managerial attention with models of noisy algorithmic screening. Relative to these verified papers, its distinctive object is the joint determination of worker search, project screening, and delegation when false positives use managerial capacity. The packet does not establish global novelty.
