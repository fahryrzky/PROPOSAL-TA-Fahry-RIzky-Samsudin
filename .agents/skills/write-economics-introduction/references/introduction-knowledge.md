# Introduction Knowledge

Use this reference to understand what an economics introduction is trying to accomplish and why the recommended practices work. Apply it together with the evidence protocol, the composition reference, and the literature-positioning reference. This document supplies writing judgment; it does not replace claim-level evidence checks.

## 1. The introduction's real job

Write for an intelligent but busy reader who will initially skim. The introduction succeeds when that reader can recover:

- the concrete economic question;
- the paper's answer;
- the basis on which the answer should be believed or interpreted;
- the paper's genuine addition to existing knowledge; and
- the boundary of that addition.

This reader model explains the guide's inverted-pyramid principle. The most decision-relevant information belongs near the front because editors, referees, and researchers decide quickly whether the paper merits further attention. Background and detail become useful only after the reader knows what they illuminate.

Use this counterfactual as a demanding diagnostic: could a previously unfamiliar economist read the opening two paragraphs and accurately explain the paper's core to a colleague? If the answer is only a broad topic, a policy area, or a list of related papers, the opening has not yet done its job.

Do not confuse accessibility with simplification of the evidence. The introduction should make the paper easy to enter, not make a qualified result sound broader or cleaner than it is.

## 2. Front-load the paper without forcing a formula

Interpret “state the conclusion first” functionally. It means that the question, answer, and contribution should appear before long background, literature, technical detail, or defenses. It does not mean that every paper must begin with `We find`, or that a motivating tension can never precede the answer.

A strong opening may first present a real puzzle, obstacle, fact, or contrast and then move quickly to the paper. This is still front-loading when the entry point is itself information-rich and the answer follows while the reader is still in the opening movement.

Avoid turning the principle into a fixed six-paragraph sequence. A useful functional progression is often:

> honest tension or obstacle → question → answer → credibility → results story → changed understanding

Treat that progression as a reasoning scaffold. Merge, split, or reorder functions when the economic argument requires it. The standard is whether each move prepares the next one and whether the paper's answer remains visible.

Background should serve the finding. Retain only the background a reader needs to understand the question, interpret the evidence, or see why the answer matters. Move the rest later, compress it, or omit it.

## 3. Choose an honest opening

Select the opening from the paper's strongest source-authorized entry point. The finished guide identifies three recurring forms:

1. a counterintuitive or consequential fact;
2. an unresolved economic puzzle;
3. a genuine tension between facts, views, predictions, or institutions.

A particularly credible design, a new measurement opportunity, or a previously unavailable dataset can also provide the natural entry when that is what makes the question answerable.

### Why the hook matters

The hook is not decoration. It gives the reader a reason to ask the same question as the paper. A useful hook therefore creates a clean transition into the research task. If the hook could introduce many unrelated papers, it is probably generic rather than functional.

### Conditions for using a hook

Use a hook when all three conditions hold:

- it is supported by the manuscript or a verified source;
- it reveals the actual economic uncertainty or opportunity addressed by the paper; and
- the next movement can connect it to the research question without a conceptual jump.

Do not manufacture drama. A modest but precise empirical obstacle is stronger than an unsupported claim that the topic is a defining global challenge. A bold statement is useful only when the paper actually substantiates it.

### Common opening failures

- **Generic importance:** `X is an important economic issue.` Repair it by naming the specific unresolved fact, tradeoff, or obstacle.
- **Field-history throat-clearing:** `Economists have long been interested in X.` Repair it by explaining what remains unknown and what this paper establishes.
- **Policy rhetoric before evidence:** a long account of why a policy matters before the paper appears. Compress it to the fact needed to understand the study.
- **Decorative anecdote:** an engaging story with no analytical role. Replace it with the paper's real empirical or theoretical tension.
- **Unsupported prevalence:** `In many markets`, `widely`, or `often` without a source. Narrow the sentence to the studied setting or verify the broader claim.
- **Empty self-reference:** opening with `This paper...` when the sentence merely announces a topic. If self-reference is efficient, make it carry the concrete task or answer.

## 4. State the question and answer early

Move from the opening into a specific research question as soon as the reader has the minimum context needed to understand it. The guide's practical benchmark is to make the question identifiable by the end of the second paragraph. Treat this as a strong default, not a paragraph-count law.

State the question at the level of the actual paper. A useful formulation normally reveals the core object, relationship or task, and the outcomes or economic margins that define the paper. Include setting or design information only when it changes what the question means.

Avoid `This paper studies the effect of X on Y` when the paper distinguishes populations, margins, institutions, or estimands that the generic phrasing erases. Specificity helps the reader and disciplines the author by exposing the paper's boundary.

Connect the question to the answer. Readers care about what the paper establishes, not only what it examines. Do not postpone all results as though the paper were a mystery. Give the central direction, distinction, proposition, or answer early enough that subsequent method and background explain something already worth understanding.

When the answer is conditional, mixed, or unresolved, preserve that structure. An ambiguous net effect, a model-dependent result, or a qualified null is still an answer; smoothing it into a categorical conclusion is not.

## 5. Explain how the paper earns its answer

Give enough design, data, measurement, model, or institutional information for the reader to understand why the answer is credible and what kind of answer it is. The amount should respond to the credibility burden rather than a fixed sentence limit.

For a transparent randomized design, naming the assignment and relevant comparison may be sufficient. A non-obvious instrument, complex quasi-experiment, structural model, new measurement procedure, or historical inference may need more explanation because the interpretation depends on it.

Prefer the actual source of information to a method label:

- explain the variation rather than merely saying `instrumental variables`;
- explain what is measured and how rather than merely saying `a new dataset`;
- explain the model's identifying or economic role rather than merely saying `a structural model`;
- explain the key theoretical tension rather than merely saying `we develop a model`.

Technical detail belongs only when removing it would change the reader's understanding of the claim. Leave estimators, full assumptions, robustness sequences, and implementation detail to the body unless one of them defines the paper's contribution or its principal limitation.

## 6. Present a results story, not a result inventory

Select the smallest set of findings that allows the reader to retell the paper's argument. Arrange them so that each finding changes what the next one means. A common progression moves from the central effect or proposition to the evidence that explains, qualifies, or interprets it.

For empirical results with meaningful quantities, provide direction, magnitude, unit, comparison, population, and horizon as needed for interpretation. A bare statement of statistical significance contains little economic information. Translate the principal estimate into a scale a reader can understand when the manuscript supports that translation.

The guide's advice that readers should remember at least one number is a strong applied-empirical default, not a universal output requirement. Do not invent a number for a theoretical, qualitative, or otherwise non-quantitative paper. In those cases, precision comes from the exact proposition, condition, direction, or scope.

Do not crowd the introduction with every regression, theorem, counterfactual, robustness check, heterogeneity split, or possible channel. Secondary material normally belongs in the body. Admit a second-layer result when omitting it would cause the reader to misunderstand:

- the central answer;
- the mechanism status;
- an economically important tradeoff;
- a defining boundary or null;
- why the contribution differs from prior work.

This is an explanatory test, not an interest test. `Interesting` is not enough.

Keep result language at the evidence level recorded in the paper ledger. Separate treatment effects from supportive mechanism patterns, observed quantities from model-implied objects, and descriptive relationships from causal claims.

## 7. State a concrete and proportionate contribution

Make the contribution inspectable. `This paper contributes to the literature` tells the reader nothing. Identify the actual addition: data, measurement, method, model, mechanism, setting with analytical value, integration of previously separate margins, or evidence that changes an existing judgment.

A strong contribution statement answers two questions:

1. What is different here?
2. Why does that difference change what readers can know, distinguish, predict, or evaluate?

Specificity makes a contribution contestable, which is a virtue. It allows a referee to evaluate the claim. Vagueness may sound safer, but it prevents the reader from seeing the paper's value.

Keep the scale proportionate. Do not describe the work as revolutionary, definitive, or transformative. Do not use novelty formulas as substitutes for a verified comparison. A modest, exact contribution is more credible than an inflated one.

Use `literature-positioning-and-contributions.md` to build and verify the comparison. Use the positioning ledger for claims about previous work; use the paper ledger for what the manuscript actually delivers.

## 8. Position the paper after readers understand it

Let readers understand the paper before asking them to compare it with other papers. A long literature list in the second paragraph usually fails because readers do not yet know which differences matter.

This is a sequencing default, not a ban on early citations. A verified fact, benchmark, or unresolved disagreement may be needed to create the opening tension. What should be delayed is the full comparison of the paper with neighboring work.

Organize literature as an intellectual relationship rather than an author-by-author inventory. Show what prior work established, what relevant question or distinction remains, and how the present evidence changes that position. Treat prior authors as participants in a conversation, not opponents who must be diminished.

See `literature-positioning-and-contributions.md` for the complete workflow and failure modes.

## 9. Use a roadmap only when it helps

A roadmap is optional. Its presence in many published papers is evidence that some journals and readers find it useful, not evidence that every introduction needs one.

Include a roadmap when the paper's structure is complex, the sequence of models or evidence is not obvious, or target-journal practice makes it useful. Omit it when the structure is conventional, the introduction is already long, or the paragraph would only repeat section labels.

When included, describe the substantive task of each section. `Section 3 reports results` adds little; `Section 3 uses randomized roommate assignment to separate two peer-effect margins` helps readers navigate.

Keep the roadmap short. It should not become a second abstract or a defense of the paper's organization.

## 10. Adapt the functions to the paper type

Preserve the introduction's functions while changing how they are realized.

### Causal empirical papers

Center the economic question, actual source of identifying variation, main estimand, magnitude, and causal boundary. Explain additional identification detail only to the extent that credibility is otherwise opaque.

### Associational, descriptive, and measurement papers

Do not borrow causal verbs from the importance of the question. Emphasize the newly measured object, data coverage, comparison, descriptive pattern, or measurement solution and explain what becomes knowable.

### Structural and quantitative papers

Explain the economic tradeoff, why the model is needed, which objects data discipline, and what the main counterfactual establishes. Mark model-implied behavior and welfare conclusions as such. Do not present estimated model objects as directly observed facts.

### Theory papers

Lead with the puzzle, mechanism, or failure of an existing benchmark. State the main proposition and the conditions that create it. Do not force a numerical magnitude or empirical identification paragraph when neither exists.

### Historical papers

Give only the institutional context needed to understand the question and evidentiary opportunity. Distinguish documented history, empirical inference, and broader interpretation.

### Mixed papers

Choose one organizing spine. Do not write a mini-introduction for every component. Explain how theory, measurement, empirics, or structural analysis jointly answer one question.

## 11. Draft early and rewrite late

Use an early rough introduction to clarify the research story. Writing exposes whether the question, answer, and contribution actually fit together. Do not treat that rough version as authoritative after the research changes.

Rewrite the introduction after the body stabilizes. The body often changes the interpretation of the main result, the importance of a mechanism, or the scale of the contribution. A late rewrite is therefore part of research accuracy, not cosmetic polishing.

During revision:

- trace every substantive sentence to the appropriate ledger;
- remove promises the body does not fulfill;
- narrow contributions the literature record does not support;
- move background behind the claim it explains;
- delete repetition and traffic-direction prose;
- read the opening as a first-time reader rather than as the paper's author;
- test whether paragraphs advance one argument rather than collect related facts.

Do not let polishing authorize factual drift. A smoother synonym can silently broaden the outcome, setting, mechanism, or contribution.

## 12. Common failure modes and repairs

| Failure | Why it fails | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| The paper appears only after a page of context | Readers cannot tell what the context is for | Move the question and answer forward; retain only context needed to interpret them |
| The opening claims universal importance | Importance is asserted rather than demonstrated | Use a verified fact, puzzle, obstacle, or precise consequence |
| The hook is disconnected from the analysis | It promises a different paper | Replace it with the study's real economic entry point or add the missing bridge |
| The question is broad and the answer vague | Readers cannot recover the estimand or boundary | Name the actual object, relationship, population, and answer |
| The method is a label | A label does not explain credibility | State the variation, measurement solution, model role, or evidentiary opportunity |
| Results are a numbered inventory | Equal emphasis obscures the paper's spine | Keep the first-order result and only explanatory second-layer findings |
| Results rely on `significant` or adjectives | Readers cannot judge economic content | Supply exact direction, magnitude, comparison, or proposition when supported |
| Mechanism language outruns the evidence | The introduction becomes more certain than the paper | Restore the source's evidence level and separate effects from interpretations |
| Contributions are generic or inflated | Neither value nor scope can be evaluated | State the exact increment and why it matters; remove self-evaluation |
| Literature appears as a name list | The paper's intellectual position remains unclear | Organize sources around the comparison needed to understand the contribution |
| The roadmap repeats headings | It consumes attention without navigation value | Name substantive section tasks or omit the roadmap |
| The introduction copies the abstract | It compresses rather than develops the argument | Build a progressive explanation of motivation, credibility, results, and position |
| The introduction promises more than the body | Referees will detect the mismatch | Treat the body as authority and revise every unsupported promise |
| A published-sample pattern becomes a template | Frequency is mistaken for necessity | Use sample patterns as calibration and preserve paper-specific judgment |

## 13. Final reader tests

Before delivery, ask:

1. Can a reader identify the concrete question and answer from the opening movement?
2. Does the opening create interest with information rather than rhetoric?
3. Does every background sentence help interpret a later claim?
4. Is the source of credibility concrete enough for this design or paper type?
5. Do the selected findings form a causal, theoretical, or explanatory story rather than a list?
6. Are magnitude, mechanism, scope, and uncertainty expressed at exactly the supported level?
7. Is the contribution concrete, proportionate, and verified against relevant literature?
8. Does the literature discussion explain a relationship rather than display reading volume?
9. Is the roadmap useful enough to justify its space?
10. Could an unfamiliar economist retell the paper accurately after the first two paragraphs?
11. Does every promise in the introduction have a later payoff?
12. Could any paragraph be deleted without changing the reader's understanding? If so, compress or remove it.

There is no universal introduction formula. The reliable discipline is to put the paper's most consequential, supportable information first; let each paragraph answer the question created by the previous one; and revise until the reader can recover one coherent, accurate research story.
