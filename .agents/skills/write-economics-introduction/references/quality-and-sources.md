## 1. Hard gates

An introduction cannot pass if it fails any item below. A hard-gate failure is an automatic rejection regardless of fluency, structure, or total points.

### Manuscript-truth gate

- Invented, unsupported, or contradicted research question, fact, result, method, design, model, mechanism, robustness result, limitation, implication, or contextual claim.
- An unsupported claim span hidden inside an otherwise supported sentence, including a compound noun, modifier, category relation, prepositional explanation, causal clause, intuition, transition, or future-research prescription.
- Wrong number, sign, unit, baseline, denominator, estimand, population, institution, period, outcome, or horizon.
- Association presented as causation; a structural implication or counterfactual presented as a reduced-form causal estimate; a theoretical result presented as empirical validation.
- A supportive, consistent-with, suggestive, or model-implied mechanism presented as directly identified or observed.
- A candidate mechanism substituted for the identified treatment in the hook, research question, answer, implication, contribution, literature comparison, or closing sentence; or one component of a bundled treatment presented as separately identified.
- A measured proxy silently broadened into the latent construct, or a technical object replaced by a materially different umbrella term.
- A latent construct, observed proxy, realized outcome, estimated object, or counterfactual statistic silently substituted for another member of that set.
- Assignment unit, treatment recipient, delivery actor, observation unit, or analysis unit confused in a way that changes the design.
- A source-reported secondary, exploratory, or robustness result promoted to the introduction's primary identity.
- A result, interview, mechanism, robustness exercise, validation, or limitation assigned a research-status label that the source does not use.
- Geographic, institutional, occupational, population, or temporal scope expanded beyond the manuscript.
- A broad prevalence, importance, urgency, or controversy claim without manuscript or cited-source support.
- An unverified but plausible industry, regional, institutional, or policy fact introduced from general knowledge rather than the defined evidence universe.
- A newly calculated magnitude or benchmark whose conversion inputs, population, horizon, or arithmetic are absent, unchecked, or wrong.
- An unreported theoretical or structural primitive, information set, timing step, action, contract term, constraint, equilibrium response, proof intuition, or condition added because it is conventional or would explain the result.
- A model assumption turned into a claim about real-world prevalence or trends, or a reported model result given a proof intuition or mechanism the source does not supply.
- An illustrative confounder, omitted variable, behavioral channel, implementation detail, or alternative explanation not supplied by the manuscript, even if offered only as an example.
- `Robust`, `stable`, `precise`, or an equivalent characterization without reported robustness or uncertainty evidence.
- A size, importance, efficiency, cost, stability, welfare, or policy evaluation without an author statement or explicit comparison basis.
- A fixed effect, clustering choice, instrument, pretrend, or selection pattern given a study-specific interpretation not authorized by the source.
- Observation frequency, seasonality, remaining identifying variation, or an estimating equation inferred from a time fixed-effect label alone.
- An exact source category converted into an unverified parent, child, overlap, or mutual-exclusion relation.
- A source-scoped null restated without its selected sample, proxy, horizon, power, precision, or detection boundary.
- A future-research prescription, external-validity warning, or policy implication absent from the manuscript and verified positioning sources.

### Citation-truth gate

- Invented author, year, title, outlet, citation key, quotation, finding, method, or literature fact.
- A cited source does not support the adjacent substantive clause or is available only at an evidence level insufficient for the characterization.
- A bibliography-only reference treated as if its full argument, design, mechanism, or limitation had been verified.
- A citation cluster used to support a synthetic claim that its members do not jointly establish.
- A material source-version conflict ignored or the wrong version characterized as authoritative.
- An unresolved citation placeholder in text presented as publication-ready.
- A finite verified source packet characterized as a `small`, `growing`, `large`, `nascent`, or otherwise distributionally described literature without separate support.
- Cross-study effect differences presented as evidence that the current paper's timing, personalization, setting, or design causes a larger or smaller response.
- A data type, randomization or identification unit, sample attribute, treatment detail, outcome, or limitation assigned to a cited study without source support, including an attribute imported from another study.

### Positioning-truth gate

- `First`, `only`, `unique`, `novel`, `little is known`, `few studies`, `understudied`, `fills a gap`, or an equivalent priority claim without evidence appropriate to its scope.
- Absence from a limited supplied bibliography presented as absence from the literature.
- A contribution asserted without both body evidence for what this paper delivers and literature evidence for the comparison.
- Prior work caricatured, dismissed, or assigned a limitation not established by the cited source.
- A claim that literatures were separate, disconnected, silent, ignored, bridged, or unified when the verified comparison universe supports only paper-specific differences.
- A claimed comparison axis differs from the actual relation between this paper and prior work.
- Several rhetorical contributions created by splitting one substantive contribution, or an author aspiration presented as a delivered advance.
- A joint model or analysis presented as proving that a result is invisible, impossible, or unattainable in every model that studies one margin separately without an explicit proposition or verified comparison.

### Promise-and-delivery gate

- An advertised result, mechanism, design capability, contribution, welfare conclusion, or policy implication that the paper body does not deliver.
- The central question, principal answer, or essential credibility source omitted while attractive secondary material dominates.
- A hook that promises a puzzle, conflict, prevalence, or institutional fact the introduction never substantiates or the paper does not address.
- A conclusion from the introduction that outruns the paper's external validity, equilibrium concept, model dependence, measurement, or welfare object.
- A blocked conflict silently resolved in favor of stronger prose.
- Unresolved placeholders or notes inside a purported final introduction.
- Formal manuscript prose delivered in the wrong language relative to the request or Skill default.
- Internal classification, readiness, spine, ledger, audit, or workflow narration exposed in draft/rewrite delivery; or manuscript prose wrapped in unrequested Markdown headings, separators, tables, or prefatory commentary.
- A roadmap, section number, or section responsibility invented without a supplied manuscript structure.
- A conservative-ready risk note that expands into an audit, repeats every rejected claim, or is materially longer than needed to identify the missing evidence and affected positioning.
- A risk note that labels an unverified, unsupported, conflicting, or unevaluated claim as contradicted, or otherwise changes its evidence status.

These gates control semantic truth, not one preferred writing style. Do not convert them into a growing list of banned surface words. Diagnose whether a phrase changes fact, scope, causal status, mechanism strength, citation support, positioning relation, centrality, or promise.

## 2. 100-point rubric

Apply this rubric only after all hard gates pass.

| Dimension | Points | Full-credit standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Manuscript fidelity and evidence calibration | 20 | Every paper claim is accurate, correctly scoped, hierarchically appropriate, and matched to causal, model, or mechanism strength |
| Citation and literature-positioning fidelity | 20 | Every cited relationship is traceable; gaps and comparisons are fair, specific, and sufficiently supported |
| Research question, motivation, and opening | 15 | The opening creates a real need for a precise question and lets the reader understand why the answer matters without generic throat-clearing |
| Contribution articulation and verifiability | 15 | Contributions are concrete, body-delivered, literature-relative, and economically meaningful without promotional inflation |
| Method, results, and boundary selection | 10 | Credibility or model logic is interpretable; a few central results form a hierarchy; only interpretation-changing qualifications enter |
| Argument structure and paragraph transitions | 10 | The introduction advances through reader dependencies; paragraphs have distinct jobs and transitions arise from ideas rather than stock labels |
| Natural academic English and information density | 5 | Prose is precise, confident but bounded, readable, non-template, and appropriately compressed |
| Mode, triggering, clarification, and delivery | 5 | The correct mode is used; blocking questions are targeted; formal output is usable; internal ledgers and process narration remain private when appropriate |

A score strictly above 95 requires no meaningful weakness in either manuscript fidelity or citation and positioning fidelity. Do not average away a weak representative case. Use the lowest representative case as the Skill score during independent acceptance.

## 3. Final quality checklist

### Opening and first movement

- Does the opening use a documented fact, tension, obstacle, puzzle, design opportunity, measurement problem, model tradeoff, or answer rather than ceremonial importance?
- If no verified hook exists, does the opening begin directly from the paper-specific task or fact rather than inventing industry background, a real-world trend, or an illustrative causal chain?
- Does the hook lead naturally to the paper's actual question?
- By the end of the opening movement, normally the first two paragraphs, can a reader state the specific question, answer, and reason to continue?
- Would removing background that precedes the answer improve the information hierarchy?

### Question, credibility, and results

- Is the research question concrete enough to define outcome, setting, mechanism, comparison, or task as appropriate?
- Does the question name the identified treatment or actual theoretical object rather than promote a candidate mechanism or component?
- Does the method description identify the variation, measurement solution, model logic, or theoretical mechanism rather than merely name a technique?
- Are the few selected findings the ones that answer the main question?
- Are magnitudes interpretable and verified when numbers materially improve understanding?
- Are nulls, mechanisms, heterogeneity, and robustness included only when they alter the central interpretation?
- Does every included null carry its scope and detection or precision boundary in the same claim?
- Would deleting each secondary, exploratory, heterogeneity, robustness, or cost detail leave the reader's understanding of the answer, credibility, and boundary unchanged? If so, delete it.

### Contributions and literature

- Can each contribution be expressed as a verified difference along a named comparison axis?
- Does the body deliver every contribution?
- Do cited sources support the description of prior work and the claimed relation?
- Does every cited-study attribute belong to that study, and is every field-wide relation bounded to the verified comparison universe?
- Is literature organized as an argument or conversation rather than an author-by-author inventory?
- Are novelty and gap phrases carrying evidence, or merely substituting for a concrete comparison?
- Does the prose acknowledge prior work fairly and avoid needing predecessors to be wrong for this paper to matter?

### Composition and length

- Does each paragraph answer a reader question, introduce necessary evidence, and create a conceptual exit to the next paragraph?
- Is the functional sequence fitted to this paper rather than copied from a standard template?
- Does any result list reproduce table order instead of telling a result story?
- Is each indispensable result, specification, and boundary stated once rather than repeated as defensive reassurance?
- Does a complex design earn additional explanation by resolving a real credibility concern?
- Is a roadmap included only when it adds navigation, and is it concise and substantive?
- Is length justified by identification, model, literature, and interpretive workload rather than repetition?

### Final truth checks

- Has every substantive clause passed output-to-ledger review?
- Has every externally testable claim span inside those clauses passed provenance review, including compound nouns, modifiers, category relations, explanatory phrases, intuition, and risk-note statements?
- Does each output span map to a source span with the same semantic role, rather than relying on a shared word between a specification component and a data-frequency claim, or between a joint model and an impossibility claim?
- Does every relational pronoun or phrase such as `this pattern`, `reverses`, or `not visible` bind to the exact supported antecedent?
- Do the opening question, effect statements, contribution passage, and closing all preserve the exact causal or theoretical subject?
- Does every evaluative modifier have an explicit source or benchmark?
- Are latent constructs, proxies, realized outcomes, estimates, and counterfactual objects kept distinct throughout?
- Have all core ledger items and necessary boundaries passed ledger-to-output review?
- Has every introduction promise been mapped to a body location?
- Are citations, bibliographic keys, and versions verified?
- Are unresolved risks reported outside rather than hidden inside formal prose?
- Does each user-facing risk retain its exact status—supported, partially supported, unsupported, unverified, contradicted, or conflicting?
- Was the complete rendered response, including any user-language note, audited after composition rather than allowing the note to bypass claim-span review?
- Does the first delivered sentence begin the manuscript rather than narrate the Skill workflow?
- In draft/rewrite mode, has the response been freshly rendered as manuscript prose rather than copied from an internal work log, with no state label, inventory, ledger, plan, audit, heading, or separator?
- Has every derived numerical interpretation been independently recomputed from source-provided inputs, or otherwise removed?
- Does every roadmap sentence map to an actual supplied section structure?
- Is the delivered artifact in the requested mode and language?

## 4. Normative and calibration layers

Keep two layers distinct:

- **Guide-derived norm:** use an inverted information hierarchy; make the opening perform real work; state a specific question and answer early; explain the source of identification or interpretation; report a few meaningful findings with magnitude when useful; make contributions specific, honest, and contestable; organize literature as a relationship; use a roadmap conditionally; rewrite the introduction after the body stabilizes.
- **Top Five calibration:** observe how these functions are realized in recent papers, including flexible order, varying design explanation, integrated or separate literature positioning, different roadmap choices, and elastic length.

The guide is the normative layer. The ten-paper sample tests and refines execution. A frequency in ten papers does not create a universal rule, and an unusual sample paper does not repeal a guide principle.

## 5. Top Five calibration

The complete ten-paper introduction sample supports these cautious observations:

- introductions are approximately 1,760–2,978 words under the project's extraction and counting method, with a median near 2,265;
- seven use a conventional roadmap, two omit one, and one integrates section navigation into substantive prose;
- a recurring functional progression is tension or obstacle, question, answer, credibility, result story, and changed understanding;
- complex empirical or quantitative designs often spend more space explaining credibility or model discipline;
- mechanisms, robustness, nulls, and heterogeneity enter when they change interpretation, not because every introduction needs those modules;
- literature and contribution placement varies, but readers usually understand the paper itself before detailed positioning carries substantial weight.

Use these observations as elastic calibration. They do not impose a minimum of 1,760 words, a maximum of 2,978 words, a mandatory roadmap, a fixed paragraph count, or a required location for literature. Target-journal conventions and the paper's argumentative load prevail.

## 6. Source integrity and provenance

Primary development sources are:

- a bilingual introduction-writing guide authored by the skill author, synthesizing the works credited in the repository NOTICE file;
- a bilingual compilation of introductions from ten published economics papers, used only as structural calibration, subject to the exception below;
- the original published versions of those same ten papers;
- Anthropic's public skill-authoring documentation (skill-creator).

The consolidated extraction for sample paper 9 is truncated and must not be treated as that paper's complete introduction. The authoritative full-text source for its introduction is the original published version of that paper. Length, sequence, roadmap, and paragraph-function statistics must use the complete source. Preserve the consolidated file unchanged and document the substitution rather than silently mixing partial and full versions.

The source guide cites Cochrane, McCloskey, Thomson, and selected economics-paper examples. Preserve the knowledge and reasoning, but do not automatically insert those citations into a user's introduction.

Do not copy calibration-paper facts, sentences, citations, or title formulas into a user's manuscript. The sample is structural evidence, not reusable prose. Keep original-paper text and workspace-specific development artifacts out of the installable Skill.

## 7. Mechanical validation boundary

A validator may reliably flag:

- unresolved placeholders;
- missing citation keys when a bibliography is supplied;
- malformed citation commands or obvious wrapper noise;
- extreme length requiring a human compression or completeness review;
- words such as `first`, `only`, or `little is known` that require evidence review;
- possible generic openings or roadmap boilerplate.

A validator cannot establish:

- whether a paper supports a claim;
- whether a citation supports its neighboring sentence;
- whether a gap is real;
- whether a contribution is new;
- whether causal or mechanism language is calibrated;
- whether the paragraph order is persuasive;
- whether the introduction fulfills its promises.

Treat script output as a review queue, not semantic proof. Do not turn elastic length, paragraph count, roadmap presence, first-person voice, or a particular opening syntax into a mechanical hard failure.
