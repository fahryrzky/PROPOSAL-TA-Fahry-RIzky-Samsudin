# Sparse-Packet Protocol

Use this protocol when the authoritative evidence for some or all manuscript claims consists only of a compact fact packet, abstract, author note, table, or local excerpt without readable supporting sections. Sparse describes evidence depth, not paper quality or readiness. A sparse packet may support an honest final introduction, but it imposes a lower ceiling on factual elaboration.

Do not trigger this protocol merely because a manuscript is short, one optional mechanism is missing, positioning is incomplete, or the user requests a short introduction. Exit it for any claim owner once full supporting text, propositions, result discussions, or verified source material becomes available. In a mixed packet, apply it claim by claim; do not suppress well-supported explanation from full manuscript sections.

## 1. Use a predicate budget, not a length target

In a sparse packet, logical coherence comes from selecting and ordering supported propositions, not from inventing premises between them. Every externally testable claim span must map directly to an explicit source proposition. This includes:

- hooks, prevalence and institutional background;
- adjectives and comparisons;
- `because`, `by`, `thereby`, purpose clauses, and behavioral chains;
- method roles and identification explanations;
- model intuition and proof logic;
- mechanism, selection, and confounder stories;
- literature meta-relations and contribution judgments;
- external validity, policy meaning, and future-research claims;
- the closing synthesis.

Purely organizational transitions are allowed only when they add no new cause, purpose, hierarchy, evaluation, scope, or factual relation. If a bridge needs an unstated premise, remove the bridge and place the two supported propositions in a simpler order.

Do not treat a compact packet as a summary whose missing conventional details may be reconstructed. Source silence remains `not established`. Do not infer implementation mechanics, variable definitions, model timing, information ownership, parameter discipline, assignment implications, fixed-effect roles, a selector type, a literature trend, or a policy audience from familiar disciplinary practice.

## 2. Build the minimum evidence-closed chain

The minimum complete introduction contains:

`accurate question → supplied data, design, model, or research task → main result or proposition → one indispensable credibility or interpretation boundary → verified positioning only when available`

Opening tension, mechanism, extended intuition, policy implications, external validity, a roadmap, and a full literature story are optional. Omit a function when no source-authorized statement can realize it. Never replace it with a generic sentence so the draft resembles a long journal introduction.

The result may be only a few paragraphs. Three to seven paragraphs is a common consequence of a compact packet, not a quota or cap. As a practical calibration, sparse formal prose is normally no longer than the source material that actually supports introduction claims; modest expansion is justified only by syntax and organization, not by new premises. Merge functions when the evidence burden is small. Do not approach the Top Five calibration range by repeating numbers, expanding limitations, or supplying background. Conversely, do not compress away the central question, answer, credibility identity, main result, or boundary needed to prevent a false reading.

Give every fact one home. State each exact magnitude, main proposition, comparison, and indispensable limitation once. Do not restate it in an opening capsule, later results paragraph, and closing. A secondary result, null, mechanism, heterogeneity pattern, cost, or extension receives space only when it changes the central answer or its boundary. Do not give a detail its own paragraph merely because it is safe to report. When verified positioning is unavailable, the introduction may end after its final supported result or boundary; do not manufacture a contribution or future-research closing.

## 3. Use the type-specific minimum

These are functional combinations, not templates or mandatory paragraph counts.

### Causal empirical

Use the supported question, exact assignment or identifying variation, identified treatment, population and outcome, main effect with scope, and at most one interpretation-changing mechanism or null boundary. Explain why the design is credible only when the source provides that explanation. Do not add a behavioral hook, balance implication, preregistration rationale, implementation advantage, scalability, cost-effectiveness, or policy meaning from convention.

### Descriptive or associational

Use the exact sample and question, measured comparison or estimate, and one local selection or noncausal boundary. Include a suggestive interpretation only in the source's own evidentiary terms. Do not define variables, explain fixed effects, name a selector or confounder, infer the remaining variation, or propose future research unless supplied.

### Structural or quantitative

Keep observed inputs, latent objects, estimated or disciplined objects, model fit, counterfactual outputs, welfare components, and maintained assumptions separately named. Select the principal counterfactual and only the boundary needed to interpret it. Do not add industry trends, regulatory actors, physical mechanisms, parameter-identification stories, proxy relations, dominance, offsets, or policy rankings not explicit in the packet. Report each headline magnitude once.

### Pure theory

Use the stated question, primitives and constraints, main proposition or comparative static, the conditions under which it holds or disappears, necessary distributional result or extension, and verified neighboring-model comparisons. Use only author-supplied intuition. If the packet gives propositions and conditions but no additional proof logic, stop there: do not reconstruct named forces, a timeline, information allocation, action sequence, dominance argument, or surplus decomposition.

### Measurement

Use the exact measured object, population and coverage, supplied construction, validation evidence, principal distribution or comparison, and interpretation boundary. Do not turn an index name into a definition, a correlation into validation, or a measured proxy into its latent construct.

### Historical

Use the documented event or question, actual archival construction and comparison, inferential status, main finding, and exact period and institution boundary. Add a mechanism only with direct evidence. Do not generalize a selected setting into a broader population or infer historical prevalence.

### Mixed

Build each inferential layer from its own authorized predicates. State what the first layer establishes, what it cannot answer only when the source says so, why the second layer is used when supplied, and the linked conclusion. Never borrow causal verbs, model mechanisms, or policy implications across layers.

## 4. Keep positioning proportional

Verified full-text summaries can support exact paper-to-paper comparisons. Titles alone cannot. A finite source packet supports only the named relations it explicitly states; it does not establish field size, trends, consensus, global gaps, or priority.

When positioning evidence is incomplete, the formal introduction may end with its final supported result or a concrete description of what the paper delivers. Do not invent a contribution universe or turn a descriptive result into a research agenda. If the user explicitly requested unsupported positioning, place one or two short sentences in the user's language after the prose, normally no more than about 50 English words or the equivalent compact Chinese length. State the missing source category and affected claim categories collectively; do not quote, number, or rebut requests one by one.

## 5. Run the sparse-source ceiling check

For each sentence, underline privately every word or phrase that asserts an external fact, relation, explanation, evaluation, or scope. Confirm that the source explicitly supplies the same owner and semantic role. Ask:

1. Did a grammatical transition introduce a factual premise?
2. Did a conventional method or model interpretation become a paper-specific claim?
3. Did a narrow object broaden when restated?
4. Did a requested intuition or contribution cause a proof or literature story to be reconstructed?
5. Did repetition, background, or a future implication enter only to make the draft feel complete?

Delete or narrow every failing span. A short source-closed introduction is the correct finished product when the evidence is compact.
