# Literature Positioning and Contributions

Use this reference when an introduction states a research gap, compares the paper with prior work, cites literature, or claims a contribution. It explains how to turn verified literature evidence into an accurate intellectual position. It assumes that the evidence protocol has already created separate paper and positioning ledgers; do not rebuild those ledgers here.

## 1. The purpose of positioning

Literature positioning should help the reader answer:

- What was already known?
- What remained unresolved, unidentified, unmeasured, unmodeled, or disputed?
- What does this paper add?
- Why does that addition change understanding?

Positioning is not a display of how many papers the author has read. It is the comparison needed to evaluate the present paper. This is why the finished guide recommends explaining the paper before giving a substantial literature review: differences are meaningful only after the reader knows what this paper does.

Treat the literature as a conversation. A paper can matter without declaring all predecessors wrong. Generous, accurate credit increases credibility and makes the real increment easier to see.

## 2. Start from the paper's delivered contribution

Determine what the manuscript body actually delivers before searching for language that makes it sound novel. A contribution is relational, but one side of the relation must be a stable description of the current paper.

Write a conservative internal statement containing:

- the paper's core task or question;
- the evidence, model, or measurement that answers it;
- the first-order result;
- the indispensable scope and boundary; and
- the candidate increment over prior work.

Map the first four elements to the paper ledger. Map only the final comparative element to the positioning ledger. This separation prevents an author's aspiration in the old introduction from being mistaken for a result delivered by the body.

If the body does not establish a claimed mechanism, welfare effect, general scope, or policy result, literature novelty cannot make that claim available. Positioning may explain why an established result matters; it cannot repair an unsupported result.

## 3. Build a contribution comparison matrix

Before composing contribution prose, compare the paper with the closest relevant work or literature strands. Use a matrix with fields such as:

| Field | Question |
|---|---|
| Prior work or strand | Which verified papers define the relevant benchmark? |
| Established knowledge | What do those sources actually show? |
| Object and outcome | Are the papers studying the same economic object and outcome? |
| Setting and population | Does the difference change interpretation or only geography? |
| Data or measurement | What becomes observable here that was not observable before? |
| Design or model | What causal, theoretical, or quantitative object can this approach recover? |
| Mechanism or margin | Does this paper distinguish a channel or margin prior work could not? |
| Result or judgment | Does the paper confirm, overturn, reconcile, bound, or qualify an existing view? |
| Scope of comparison | How broad is the verified literature search? |
| Supported contribution wording | What exact sentence does the evidence license? |
| Forbidden upgrade | Which stronger statement would exceed the evidence? |

The matrix is an internal reasoning aid, not a required user-facing table. Its purpose is to expose false comparisons. A different country is not automatically a contribution; a new dataset is not valuable merely because it is new; a method is not an advance unless it changes what can be learned.

Prefer the closest papers and the work that deserves priority. A comprehensive bibliography may be appropriate elsewhere, but the introduction normally needs the sources that define the paper's intellectual position.

## 4. Distinguish contribution families

Use the family that matches the paper rather than filling a standard list.

### New data or measurement

Explain what the data or measure makes visible and why the object matters. `We use a new dataset` is incomplete unless new coverage, linkage, frequency, outcome, population, or measurement solves a real limitation.

### New identification or empirical design

Explain which previously confounded relationship the design can distinguish and what source of variation makes that possible. Do not equate a sophisticated estimator with a contribution.

### New model or theoretical mechanism

Explain what economic behavior, equilibrium force, or logical possibility the model clarifies. State the conditions and do not imply empirical validation unless the paper supplies it.

### New mechanism or separated margin

Explain why distinguishing the mechanism or margin changes interpretation, welfare, or policy evaluation. Preserve whether the evidence identifies the mechanism, implies it within a model, or is merely consistent with it.

### New setting

A setting becomes a contribution when institutions, population, scale, history, or constraints allow a meaningful test, reveal a boundary, or change external validity. `Evidence from Z` alone is not an intellectual increment.

### New synthesis or unified framework

Explain what previously separate facts, margins, literatures, or decisions can now be evaluated together. Verify that the unified treatment is actually present in the body.

### New judgment on an existing debate

Show which view the evidence supports, rejects, reconciles, or qualifies and why the new evidence is probative. Do not create a binary debate when the literature is more nuanced.

### Boundary, null, or qualification

A carefully established limit can be a contribution when it changes a widely held expectation or defines where a mechanism applies. Preserve detection limits and scope; a null estimate is not proof of zero.

Many papers combine two families, but more listed contributions do not imply a stronger paper. Organize around one central addition and include secondary contributions only when they help explain that addition.

## 5. Organize literature as a relationship

Replace author-by-author summaries with a structured intellectual movement:

1. state what the relevant work has established;
2. identify the precise remaining question, ambiguity, or limitation;
3. explain how this paper addresses it;
4. state why the difference changes understanding.

Words such as `however` and `although` often signal the turn, but the logic matters more than the connector. Do not force a gap narrative if the paper instead confirms, unifies, transports, measures, or disciplines an existing idea.

Group papers by the analytical role they play:

- evidence establishing a benchmark;
- work using a related design or setting;
- theory that supplies a mechanism or prediction;
- papers with which the result is in tension;
- work whose limitations define the present contribution.

Give each group a proposition, not merely a citation cluster. The reader should understand why the group appears and how it affects the present paper.

Keep evaluative language neutral and specific. Instead of saying prior work `fails` or `ignores` an issue, describe what it studies and what remains outside its design or scope. Strong criticism is appropriate only when the evidence supports it and the distinction is necessary.

## 6. Use novelty and gap language proportionately

Treat `first`, `new`, `novel`, `little is known`, `no prior work`, `fills a gap`, and `the literature has overlooked` as empirical claims about a body of research. They require a defensible search scope and precise comparison.

Prefer bounded formulations when the evidence is bounded:

- `Among studies that examine ..., we are not aware of work that ...`
- `This paper adds evidence on ... by ...`
- `Relative to the closest studies, the design allows ...`
- `The analysis extends this literature to ... because this setting permits ...`

Do not use hedges such as `to our knowledge` as automatic permission for an unsupported first claim. The hedge acknowledges search limits; it does not perform the search.

Avoid global absence claims when only a small supplied reading set has been checked. A ten-paper calibration sample can reveal writing patterns, but it cannot prove that no prior paper exists.

Do not characterize the literature itself as `small`, `nascent`, `growing`, `large`, `extensive`, or `burgeoning` merely from the size of the supplied packet. Those adjectives assert a distribution or trend outside the verified comparisons. Name the verified work or use a neutral phrase such as `joins experimental work on ...`.

Distinguish these situations:

- **Verified priority:** the available literature search and sources support a narrowly scoped first claim.
- **Verified difference:** the closest papers support a concrete comparison, but not global priority.
- **Author-proposed position:** the manuscript asserts a contribution that has not been independently verified.
- **Insufficient positioning evidence:** only the paper body or a reference list is available.

Use language no stronger than the recorded situation.

## 7. Cite generously and precisely

Give credit for ideas, methods, data, findings, and priority that could otherwise appear to originate in the current paper. Generosity does not mean citing every remotely related paper; it means crediting the work that defines the comparison and avoiding strategic erasure.

Verify the proposition attached to each citation. A bibliographic entry, title, abstract keyword, or citation in the old introduction does not by itself establish that a paper supports the sentence being written.

For each citation-bearing claim, know:

- which source supports it;
- which part of the source supports it;
- whether the source establishes a result, interpretation, prediction, or context;
- whether the sentence's scope matches the source;
- whether multiple citations genuinely support the same proposition.

Do not fabricate authors, years, titles, findings, disputes, or citation keys. Do not silently repair uncertain bibliographic details by guessing. If the source content is unavailable, narrow or omit the claim and report the limitation outside the formal introduction when material.

Avoid citation dumping. A long parenthetical list without a clear proposition does not position the paper. Conversely, do not omit a close predecessor merely to make the contribution appear larger.

## 8. Decide where literature belongs

The default sequence is to establish the paper's question, answer, and approach before a substantial literature comparison. This lets readers understand why the differences matter.

Allow an early citation when it performs a necessary function:

- documents the opening fact or benchmark;
- defines an unresolved theoretical prediction;
- establishes a disagreement that creates the question;
- supplies a conventional estimate against which the paper's result is surprising.

Keep that early use focused. Do not let it grow into a literature review before the paper appears.

The contribution and literature discussion may be:

- a distinct later passage;
- integrated into contribution paragraphs;
- split across strands when the paper genuinely connects several conversations;
- partly moved to a separate literature section if journal practice or volume makes that clearer.

Choose the arrangement that minimizes repetition. If a separate section exists, the introduction still needs a concise, accurate statement of the paper's position.

## 9. Handle incomplete positioning evidence

Use the workflow state selected in the main skill.

### Direct-writing state

When both the paper body and reliable literature materials are sufficient, write the contribution and positioning directly. Still keep claims bounded to the checked evidence.

### Conservative-writing state

When the body is adequate but literature evidence is incomplete, write a body-grounded introduction and reduce the positioning claim. Suitable options include:

- describe what the paper provides without asserting priority;
- state a contribution relative only to verified nearby work;
- omit unsupported citations or gap language;
- tell the user briefly what literature evidence would permit a stronger position.

Do not replace missing positioning with generic claims such as `few studies have examined`.

### Blocking state

Pause for one targeted clarification when competing manuscript versions, contradictory results, or an unresolved central comparison would change the paper's claimed identity. A missing optional citation or preferred contribution count is not blocking.

If the user requests a provisional draft, keep uncertainty outside the formal prose and include only supported positioning inside it.

## 10. Adapt positioning to different paper types

### Causal empirical papers

Compare the source of variation, estimand, population, and outcome with close designs. Do not claim the paper identifies a mechanism merely because prior work could not.

### Associational, descriptive, and measurement papers

Emphasize coverage, measurement, documentation, or a newly visible pattern. Avoid framing the contribution as causal identification.

### Structural and quantitative papers

Compare the model's economic margins, disciplined objects, fit, and counterfactual capabilities. Distinguish an observed empirical fact from a model-enabled policy experiment.

### Theory papers

Position the paper relative to assumptions, mechanisms, equilibrium concepts, propositions, or unresolved logical tensions. A simpler or more general model is valuable only if the change yields insight.

### Historical papers

Compare archives, episodes, institutions, variation, and the historical or economic interpretation. Avoid present-day generalizations that the evidence cannot support.

### Mixed papers

Do not present separate contribution lists for every component. Explain how the components jointly resolve one intellectual problem.

## 11. Failure modes and repairs

| Failure | Why it fails | Repair |
|---|---|---|
| `This paper contributes to several literatures` followed by a list | The reader cannot see one central increment | Identify the primary comparison and organize secondary strands around it |
| `The literature has ignored X` | The absence claim is usually too broad and antagonistic | State what verified work covers and the precise remaining distinction |
| `To our knowledge, this is the first...` without a search basis | A hedge does not verify priority | Narrow to a checked comparison or remove the first claim |
| A new country is presented as self-evidently novel | Geography alone may not change knowledge | Explain the institutional or inferential value of the setting |
| A new method is treated as the contribution | Technique is confused with knowledge gained | State which object or comparison the method recovers |
| A reference list is converted into findings | Titles and citations do not establish propositions | Read the relevant source or omit the claim |
| Prior work is belittled | Hostility obscures the actual increment and loses trust | Credit what prior work establishes and describe the remaining boundary neutrally |
| Every cited paper receives one sentence | The review becomes a catalog | Group sources by analytical role and state one synthesis proposition |
| Contribution language exceeds the body | Positioning is used to manufacture a stronger paper | Re-anchor the contribution in delivered results before comparing |
| A supportive mechanism becomes a mechanism contribution | Evidence level is upgraded | Name the pattern as supportive or consistent with the channel |
| The same contribution appears in several forms | Repetition masquerades as multiple contributions | State the increment once, then explain its significance |
| Missing literature evidence is filled with boilerplate | Fluency hides an unverifiable gap | Use conservative-writing state and disclose the missing support outside the introduction |
| A finite source packet becomes a `small` or `growing` literature | Packet size is mistaken for field size | Name the verified comparisons without a distributional adjective |

## 12. Final positioning audit

Before delivery, inspect every literature and contribution sentence:

1. Does the paper body deliver the current-paper side of the comparison?
2. Does the positioning ledger support the prior-work side?
3. Is the comparison between like objects, outcomes, populations, methods, or claims?
4. Does the sentence explain why the difference matters?
5. Is a novelty or absence claim bounded to the search actually performed?
6. Does each citation support the proposition attached to it?
7. Have priority and intellectual debts been credited generously?
8. Has a setting, dataset, method, or model been mistaken for a contribution without explaining what it enables?
9. Does mechanism, causality, welfare, and external-validity language preserve the evidence level?
10. Could the contribution be stated more precisely and with less self-evaluation?
11. Would removing a literature paragraph change the reader's understanding of the paper's position? If not, compress it.
12. Is any unsupported claim being retained merely because it sounds conventional? Remove it.

The goal is not to make the paper appear maximally different from everything before it. The goal is to locate the paper exactly enough that a knowledgeable reader can see what is inherited, what is added, and why the addition is worth attention.
