# Title Quality Gate and Source Map

## 1. Hard gates

The entire delivered shortlist fails if any displayed title—including a strong alternative:

- promises an unsupported causal effect, mechanism, result, design, scope, or contribution;
- centers a secondary, robustness, or future-work result instead of the paper;
- expands beyond the studied population, institution, period, or object without support;
- uses novelty language without a defensible, narrow basis;
- omits the actual paper identity so completely that the title becomes generic;
- contains placeholders or uses the wrong formal language.
- substitutes a smoother but materially different term for the paper's actual outcome, data structure, sample, or design.
- grammatically or conceptually centers a secondary, robustness, exploratory, mechanism-support, or future-only result, even if the rationale acknowledges that weakness.
- uses a gerund or action noun to present a merely supportive, consistent-with, suggestive, or speculative mechanism as an achieved result.
- broadens an exact object, relationship, scope, causal status, mechanism level, or contribution beyond the closed claim ledger.
- has a grammatical head that is not in the frozen primary-spine claim set.
- contains a term mapped to a secondary, exploratory, robustness, mechanism-support, or future-only claim after the display-forbidden list has been frozen, even as a coordinate noun or subtitle.
- is displayed despite a rationale admitting that one of its central terms is secondary, supportive, or not the main outcome.
- foregrounds an unobserved or model-implied behavior without an explicit model/structural marker in that same title.
- expands a state, region, institution, industry, or period sample into broader national or market scope.
- fails an explicit requested candidate count when enough truthful primary-spine architecture variants can be generated.
- promotes a manuscript-reported secondary, exploratory, or robustness claim to primary/central/first-order status for any reason, including magnitude, share, precision, preregistration, interest, or candidate-count pressure.
- uses any lexical form of a source-reported nonprimary claim in the recommendation, alternatives, or rationale after the immutable status map is built.
- contains a substantive noun absent from the authorized title vocabulary or replaces the manuscript's provider/service/outcome object with a broader workforce, market, welfare, quality, or human-capital construct.
- expands municipal, state, occupational, industry, program, or other institutional scope to a broader umbrella such as public procurement or a national labor market without support.
- uses `versus`, `tradeoff`, `tension`, `offsetting`, a contrast, or a question premise without explicit relational claim IDs supporting opposition or direction.

A false promise cannot be compensated by memorability or brevity.

## 2. 100-point rubric

| Dimension | Points | Full-credit standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Truth and promise fidelity | 30 | Every substantive title element is licensed by the body and matches causal/scope strength |
| Central fit and research identity | 20 | The recommended title captures the paper's first-order object, relationship, task, or tension |
| Distinctiveness and candidate diversity | 15 | Shortlist is recognizable and uses genuinely different information structures |
| Information density and compression | 10 | Every word earns space; no necessary boundary is lost |
| Professional English and rhythm | 10 | Natural, memorable, non-promotional economics-paper titles |
| Family and context fit | 10 | Colon, question, result, design, setting, and hook forms are used only when justified |
| Mode and delivery experience | 5 | Correctly proposes, compares, rewrites, or audits and explains tradeoffs concisely |

A score above 95 requires that the recommended title survive a word-by-word promise audit and that alternatives are not cosmetic duplicates.

## 3. Final checklist

- Does every content word point to central body evidence?
- Is causal language within the design's ceiling?
- Is a mechanism strong and central enough to name?
- Is a place, industry, institution, or period doing identification or scope work?
- Does `Evidence from` pass the deletion test?
- Do colon halves perform distinct functions?
- Is a question title warranted by real, resolved tension?
- Is a result title safe without omitted qualifications?
- Is a hook naturally tied to the paper?
- Is the paper distinguishable from nearby work?
- Can any word be removed without losing truth or distinction?
- Do title, abstract, introduction, conclusion, and body identify the same paper?
- Would every alternative remain defensible if shown alone without the recommendation or rationale?
- Does each grammatical head identify the first-order paper rather than a secondary analysis?
- Did structural diversity preserve the frozen primary-identity bundle?
- Does every gerund describe a directly supported achieved action or result rather than convert a topic or tentative channel into a fact?
- Can every candidate's grammatical head be mapped to a primary-spine claim ID?

## 4. Normative and calibration layers

- **Guide-derived norm:** finalize late; carry at least a topic, finding, or tension; use title families conditionally; use `Evidence from` only when informative; cross-check the introduction; compress aggressively but put accuracy above cleverness.
- **Sample calibration:** the ten recent titles span topic, contrast, concept-plus-subtitle, setting/design, measurement, and theory–data identities. Seven use a colon, none uses a question, none reports a numeric or directional effect, and two use `Evidence from`. These observations refine candidate weighting but are not universal rules.

Do not convert `7/10 colon` into a colon preference or `0/10 questions` into a ban. The original guide's question form remains legitimate under strict conditions.

Do not copy a calibration title's formula or facts into a user's paper.
