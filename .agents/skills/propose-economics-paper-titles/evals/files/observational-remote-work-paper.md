# Working paper materials: Remote-Work Adoption and Firm Output

> **Note:** This is a synthetic teaching and testing packet. All names, data, results, and citations are fictional.

The paper uses a 2017–2024 panel of 320 business-service firms with at least 50 employees. Firms voluntarily chose remote-work adoption. Firm fixed-effects estimates associate adoption with 6 percent higher output per employee, but output was rising for two years before adoption. The paper explicitly rejects a causal interpretation. Employment and wages rise; turnover falls imprecisely. A survey in 46 firms finds higher schedule control but cannot identify a mechanism. There is no office-cost or welfare data. The contribution is a linked firm-worker panel describing the joint evolution of remote-work policy and outcomes. An old title, “Remote Work Causes Productivity Growth: Evidence from the United States,” overstates causality and uses an uninformative location tag.
