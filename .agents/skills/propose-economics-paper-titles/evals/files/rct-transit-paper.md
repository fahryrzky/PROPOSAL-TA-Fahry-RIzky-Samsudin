# Working paper materials: Transit Subsidies and Secondary-School Persistence

> **Note:** This is a synthetic teaching and testing packet. All names, data, results, and citations are fictional.

The paper studies 2,400 grade 11–12 students in 18 public high schools in one metropolitan district. Students were randomized within school and grade to receive a $30 monthly public-transit card. Assignment increased attendance by 2.4 percentage points from an 86 percent control mean and on-time graduation by 3.1 percentage points from a 74 percent mean. Effects are concentrated among students with commutes longer than 45 minutes. Card use is concentrated on school-day commuting hours, which is consistent with a transportation-liquidity mechanism but does not separately identify it. The setting is one urban district over two cohorts. The paper does not estimate welfare or establish that universal transit subsidies are optimal.
