# Climate/Environmental Economics Writing Style

**Source Papers:** Barwick et al. ("The Value of Pollution Information"), He, Wang & Zhang ("Watering Down Environmental Regulation in China")
**Typical Journals:** Quarterly Journal of Economics, American Economic Review, Journal of Political Economy, Journal of Development Economics, Journal of Environmental Economics and Management
**Field Code:** `climate`

---

## Structural Blueprint (9 Elements)

### 1. Opening: High-Stakes Narrative Hook

Start with a vivid, concrete fact or policy event. Immediately state the gap and why it matters for policy. Frame your study as the first to answer a crucial question.

**Formula:**
> [Concrete fact/policy event]. However, little is known about [knowledge gap]. Filling this gap is important because [policy stakes]...

**Examples:**
- "In 2013, China launched a landmark program to monitor air quality... little is known about the effects of collecting and disseminating pollution information on the daily lives of citizens in developing countries."
- "In developing countries... billions of people live under extreme pollution every day... little is known about the economic costs of alleviating pollution in these settings."

### 2. Framing Contributions: Explicit, Numbered, Sweeping

Insert a "We make X contributions" paragraph after motivation. Number them. Use strong superlatives ("first," "most comprehensive"). Explicitly contrast with existing work.

**Formula:**
> "We make three main contributions. First, to the best of our knowledge, our study provides the first [estimate/evidence] of [X]... Second, our study shows that [Y] is a crucial determinant of [Z]... Third, this study contributes to the broad empirical literature on [topic]..."

### 3. Research Design: The "Unique Setting" and Causal Language

Phrase identification as "exploiting" an institutional quirk. Emphasize exogeneity and testability. Use phrases like "plausibly exogenous," "sharp and sudden change," "as good as random," "orthogonality conditions that can be tested."

**Formula:**
> "Exploiting [institutional feature]... This empirical strategy is related in spirit to recent work by [X], but is novel in that it uses [unique setting]..."

### 4. Data: "Most Comprehensive" and "Novel" Assembly

Proudly detail data work. Emphasize uniqueness, scale, granularity. Detail merging of multiple datasets, geocoding, sample construction.

**Formula:**
> "To the best of our knowledge, the data we have compiled are the most comprehensive and disaggregated collection ever assembled on [topic]..."

### 5. Results: The "Cascade" of Evidence

Results are woven into a detective-like story. Each finding builds on the previous one. Walk the reader through the most important figure first, then tables. Decompose effects into sub-categories. Use event-study graphs to show dynamics. Always test for pre-trends.

**Formula:**
> "We begin the empirical analysis by visualizing our main findings. Figure [X] plots [Y] against [Z]... We see a sharp change at precisely [location]... To better understand the dynamics..."

### 6. Mechanisms and Decomposition: Unpacking the Black Box

After the main effect, ask "What drives this result?" Test heterogeneity by subgroups (age, cause, deferrable vs. non-deferrable, firm size, political incentives). Link outcome gradient to intermediate action.

**Formula:**
> "How do [agents] respond to [treatment]? We examine the channels... [Specific test A]... [Specific test B]... both [channel 1] and [channel 2] contributed to [outcome]."

### 7. Political Economy and Institutional Detail

Devote a subsection to "Institutional Background." Explain bureaucratic incentives, career concerns, historical context. Test for heterogeneous effects based on those incentives.

**Formula:**
> "This political economy interpretation is strongly supported by a rich set of empirical results: i. [Finding 1]... ii. [Finding 2]... iii. [Finding 3]... iv. [Finding 4]..."

### 8. Cost-Benefit or Back-of-the-Envelope Calculations

Close empirical section with a striking calculation translating a coefficient into societal dollar value. Be transparent about assumptions. State if figure is "conservative," "a lower bound," or "an upper bound."

**Formula:**
> "A back-of-the-envelope calculation for [scope] suggests that [policy] cost [X amount] per year in [metric]... Based on this estimated [parameter], our calculation suggests that [total economic magnitude]."

### 9. Conclusion: Broad Lessons, Limitations, Future Research

Brief but powerful. Draw implications for other developing countries. List 2-3 specific limitations and directions for future work.

**Formula:**
> "[Country]'s experience offers an important lesson for other developing countries... We point out some limitations of our study... [Specific direction 1] and [Specific direction 2] will be important areas for future research."

## Stylistic Micro-Elements

- **Precise quantitative phrasing:** "more than 24% lower TFP," "an order of magnitude," "1.4 percentage point decrease," "57% reduction"
- **Economic jargon defined in context:** "the shopping-pollution gradient (∂Outdoor Activities/∂Pollution)," "mortality-pollution elasticity"
- **Causal transition words:** "triggered," "led to," "driven by," "exploiting," "generated"
- **Confidence without overstatement:** "We find strong evidence that...", "The pattern is robust across...", "the estimates are conservative"
- **Appendix as strategic partner:** Push theoretical models, extra robustness, and data details to extensive online appendix

## Phrase Bank

| Function | Climate Econ Phrases |
|----------|---------------------|
| Opening hook | "In [year], [country] launched a landmark program to..." / "In developing countries, billions of people live under..." |
| Contribution claim | "To the best of our knowledge, our study provides the first..." / "the most comprehensive [X] yet compiled" |
| Identification | "Exploiting the spatial discontinuity in..." / "The basis of our identification strategy is the staggered introduction of..." |
| Results cascade | "The changes in [X] triggered a cascade of behavioral changes..." / "We begin by visualizing our main findings" |
| Mechanism | "The effect was concentrated among [subgroup]..." / "How do [agents] respond to [treatment]? We examine the channels..." |
| Cost-benefit | "A back-of-the-envelope calculation suggests..." / "[X] billion per year in [metric]" |
| Conclusion | "[Country]'s experience offers an important lesson for other developing countries..." |

## Checklist for Climate Econ Style

- [ ] Opening hooks with a vivid fact or policy event
- [ ] Contributions are numbered and use superlatives ("first," "most comprehensive")
- [ ] Identification framed as "exploiting" an institutional feature
- [ ] Data described as comprehensive/novel with detail on merging and geocoding
- [ ] Results presented as a cascade: figure first → tables → decomposition → dynamics
- [ ] Mechanisms section tests specific channels with subgroups
- [ ] Political economy/institutional background section included
- [ ] Back-of-the-envelope calculation translating coefficients to dollar values
- [ ] Conclusion draws lessons for other countries
- [ ] Technical details pushed to online appendix
