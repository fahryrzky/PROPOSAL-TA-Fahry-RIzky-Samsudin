# Literature search and story extraction

Read this for every Nature Story task.

## Purpose

The literature search serves two different questions:

1. **What is already known?** This constrains novelty and claims.
2. **How have strong papers made similar problems general-interest?** This supplies transferable story moves.

Do not confuse the second with evidence for the first.

## Query construction

Translate the raw idea into a query ledger:

| Block | Examples |
|---|---|
| System | data centres, cities, power grids, transport, households |
| Driver/intervention | warming, electrification, standard, subsidy, pollution control |
| Outcome | ozone, mortality, demand, reliability, inequality, emissions |
| Mechanism | threshold, rebound, behavioural adaptation, spillover, lock-in |
| Consequence | policy effectiveness, design failure, welfare, justice, resilience |

Search topic terms first, then combine them with one mechanism or consequence term at a time. Avoid queries that bake the desired conclusion into the search.

## Three-neighbour search

### Topic neighbours

Search the same system and outcome. These papers answer whether the direct contribution is already known.

### Mechanism neighbours

Search the proposed mechanism across adjacent systems. A threshold mechanism in buildings may have stronger precedents in power systems or heat-health studies.

### Narrative neighbours

Search general story moves: unintended consequence, rebound, trade-off, policy mix, leakage, spatial transfer, unequal exposure, sign reversal, non-stationarity, compound risk, stranded asset, and adaptation limit.

## Source priorities

1. Primary publisher pages, DOI/Crossref records, and bibliographic databases.
2. Full paper or accepted manuscript when lawfully accessible.
3. Abstract and figures when full text is unavailable; label the limited access.
4. Reviews, editorials, and research-writing commentary for interpretation only.

Start with Nature and Science families because the user asked for those writing comparators, but add the most scientifically relevant field papers. Do not force a weak prestige match.

Aim for 6–12 close papers for an ordinary architecture task. Include a mixture of recent and seminal work. Stop when new results no longer change the novelty map or story options.

## Reference matrix

Return this before recommending a story:

| Paper | Why it is close | Story opening | Tension/turn | Mechanism | Consequence | Transferable move | Difference from this idea | Access |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

“Access” must distinguish full text, abstract/metadata, and secondary summary. Provide DOI or stable publisher link. Verify title, authors, year, journal, and DOI before presenting them as facts.

## Reading protocol

For the closest 3–5 papers, inspect title, abstract, introduction opening and gap, results sequence/figure logic, and discussion opening/limits when accessible. Extract:

- the default expectation the paper establishes;
- the anomaly, tension, or unmet decision need;
- the minimum claim chain;
- where the mechanism is demonstrated rather than asserted;
- how scope expands from case to general principle;
- how consequence is quantified;
- what the paper deliberately leaves out;
- what evidence would collapse its story.

Paraphrase. Short quotations are only for exact wording analysis and must be cited.

## Novelty map

Classify each proposed contribution:

- already established;
- established in another system but untested here;
- suggested but not mechanistically resolved;
- unquantified at decision-relevant scale;
- genuinely open based on the retrieved set;
- uncertain because search coverage or access is limited.

Use cautious language: a targeted search cannot prove global novelty.

## Failure handling

- If exact-topic papers are scarce, broaden the system while holding the mechanism fixed.
- If only low-quality matches appear, say so and use mechanism neighbours rather than pretending closeness.
- If publisher access is blocked, use verified metadata/abstracts and label interpretation limits.
- If the idea is too broad, search one plausible formulation and show how results would differ under alternatives.
- If no live search tool is available, do not invent references. Provide provisional queries and mark the architecture as literature-unverified.
