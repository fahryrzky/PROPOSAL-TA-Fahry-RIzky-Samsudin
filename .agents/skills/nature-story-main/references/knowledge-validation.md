# Independent knowledge validation

Read this for every Nature Story task in which the user supplies scientific knowledge, references, mechanisms, numerical claims, policy interpretations, or writing advice.

## Principle

The user is the source of the research problem, constraints, data access, and intent. The user is not automatically the source of truth about the field. Preserve collaboration while maintaining epistemic independence.

Do not choose between blind acceptance and blanket scepticism. Convert the supplied material into claims, test them proportionally, and show what survives.

## Claim decomposition

Split the user's input into atomic claims and assign a type:

- **Definition** — what a term or metric means.
- **Empirical** — what has been observed or estimated.
- **Mechanistic** — why a response occurs.
- **Causal** — whether an intervention or driver produces an outcome.
- **Projective** — what will happen under future conditions.
- **Normative** — what policy or design should optimize.
- **Meta-scientific** — what a journal, editor, or field supposedly prefers.

Record the implied spatial scale, temporal scale, population, system boundary, and certainty. Many apparent errors are actually unspoken scope changes.

## Independent learning pass

Before using the user's claims to shape a story, learn enough of the field to evaluate them:

1. Identify canonical definitions and measurement conventions.
2. Identify accepted mechanisms and major competing explanations.
3. Identify what evidence the field considers mechanistic or causal.
4. Identify known scale dependence, boundary conditions, and heterogeneity.
5. Identify recent findings that revised older assumptions.
6. Identify relevant engineering constraints, institutions, behaviours, and affected groups.

Prioritize primary research, authoritative technical documentation, standards, assessments, and high-quality reviews. Use writing advice only for communication structure, never as evidence that a scientific claim is true.

## Triangulation

For consequential claims, seek at least two independent evidence routes when feasible:

- primary study plus review or assessment;
- observations plus model or experiment;
- multiple research groups or settings;
- technical standard plus empirical implementation evidence;
- evidence supporting the claim plus the strongest plausible contradiction.

One paper can establish that a result exists; it rarely establishes universality.

## Status labels

| Status | Meaning | Required response |
|---|---|---|
| Supported | Evidence and scope align | Use with citation and boundary |
| Partly supported | Core idea holds but wording or scope is too broad | Narrow or qualify |
| Disputed | Credible evidence supports competing interpretations | Present both and identify discriminating test |
| Outdated | Later evidence or standards materially changed the claim | Replace with current understanding |
| Incorrect | Evidence contradicts the claim within the stated scope | Correct explicitly |
| Normative | Depends on values, objectives, or affected groups | State the objective and trade-offs |
| Unresolved | Evidence or access is insufficient | Preserve uncertainty and avoid building the story on it |

## Conflict handling

When the user's statement conflicts with evidence:

1. quote or paraphrase the exact claim being assessed;
2. state whether the conflict concerns fact, mechanism, scope, terminology, or values;
3. provide the strongest relevant evidence and its limitations;
4. explain how the correction changes the story architecture;
5. retain an alternative candidate only if a decisive analysis could still support it.

Be direct without being adversarial. Do not hide disagreement to preserve a preferred narrative.

## Learning without overreach

The independent learning pass is targeted, not an unlimited literature review. Stop when additional sources no longer change:

- the definition or mechanism map;
- the status of user-provided claims;
- the novelty assessment;
- the ranking of candidate stories;
- the analyses needed to discriminate among them.

Report search and access limitations. If verification is impossible, label the affected story provisional.

## Required output

When user-provided knowledge materially shapes the project, include:

| User-provided claim | Type | Status | Independent evidence | Scope or conflict | Story consequence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then summarize:

- useful knowledge retained;
- claims corrected or narrowed;
- unresolved issues;
- disciplinary features that changed the initial framing;
- what evidence would reverse the judgement.
