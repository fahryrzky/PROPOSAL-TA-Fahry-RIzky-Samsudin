# Story archetypes for coupled engineering systems

These are hypothesis generators, not decorative labels. Use an archetype only when the idea contains its signal and the proposed analysis can distinguish it from alternatives.

| Archetype | Scientific move | Signals | Decisive analysis | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hidden dependency | Recast an environmental or social condition as a resource the engineered system depends on | Design assumes a background condition is free or stable | quantify dependence, perturb the condition, establish boundary | metaphor without measurable dependence |
| Non-stationary design mismatch | Long-lived infrastructure is designed on a historical distribution that shifts during its lifetime | fixed thresholds, standards, return periods, design climate | vintage-aware exposure; historical vs future design counterfactual | projection alone without design consequence |
| Threshold or regime transition | Small driver changes cause disproportionate responses near an operating boundary | cut-offs, saturation, switching, compliance thresholds | response curve, breakpoint uncertainty, perturbation/attribution | post-hoc threshold hunting |
| Trade-off or co-benefit | Improving one objective changes another | coupled emissions, cost, health, reliability, land, water | common baseline, multiple objectives, Pareto frontier, affected groups | counting two outcomes without a real conflict |
| Rebound or behavioural feedback | Efficiency or policy changes behaviour, partially offsetting the intended benefit | demand elasticity, moral licensing, induced travel/use, producer response | behavioural counterfactual; direct and system-wide effects | calling any offset a rebound |
| Bottleneck relocation | Solving one constraint moves the limiting factor elsewhere | interconnected sectors or supply chains | constraint decomposition before/after intervention | boundary chosen to hide the new bottleneck |
| Spatial spillover, leakage, or burden shifting | Local gains create external losses elsewhere | trade, grids, atmospheric transport, commuting, relocation | origin–destination accounting or spatial causal design | confusing correlation with transfer |
| Temporal reversal | Short-term improvement differs from long-term response | emergency controls, adaptation, technology turnover | aligned short/long counterfactuals and mechanism | mixing incomparable periods |
| Sign reversal or context dependence | The same intervention has opposite effects across climates, places, or groups | interacting background states | interaction surface and mechanistic attribution | subgroup fishing without prior logic |
| Distributional divergence | Aggregate improvement conceals unequal benefits or harms | exposure, income, race/ethnicity, occupation, access | distributional metrics, uncertainty, baseline vulnerability | equity as an afterthought |
| Compound risk or interaction | Joint drivers create non-additive outcomes | co-occurring hazards or interventions | factorial design; interaction term; joint extremes | merely overlaying two maps |
| Policy-mix non-additivity | Combined instruments succeed or fail differently from their parts | overlapping incentives, institutions, objectives | factorial policy counterfactuals, implementation constraints | assuming additivity |
| Lock-in and path dependence | Early choices constrain later options and costs | durable capital, learning, networks, sunk costs | vintage/transition pathways and alternative timing | generic “lock-in” language |
| Scale reversal | A relationship changes from device to system, neighbourhood to region, or mean to extreme | aggregation, feedback, network coupling | harmonized multi-scale analysis | comparing inconsistent definitions |
| Assumption inversion | A common strategic belief changes when system boundaries or incentives change | entrenched framing or simplified game | formal alternative framing with empirical/model evidence | straw-man expectation |

## How to compose archetypes

Combine at most two primary archetypes in the central spine. A third can be a consequence. Useful combinations include:

- non-stationary mismatch → threshold response → infrastructure consequence;
- intervention → spatial spillover → distributional divergence;
- efficiency gain → behavioural rebound → policy redesign;
- compound drivers → sign reversal → climate-specific intervention;
- policy mix → non-additivity → win–win region.

Avoid “archetype soup”: if each figure requires a different central concept, the paper probably lacks narrative economy.

## Required proof language

For any chosen archetype, state:

1. the reference condition;
2. the causal or mechanistic pathway;
3. the observable signature unique enough to test it;
4. the most plausible competing explanation;
5. the result that would falsify or materially weaken the story.
