# Domain lenses

Use only the lenses relevant to the user's system. Their purpose is to surface omitted mechanisms and affected groups.

## Atmosphere and air pollution

- Chemistry–meteorology coupling, precursor limitation, secondary formation, deposition, and transport.
- Mean exposure versus extremes, episodes, seasonality, and threshold exceedances.
- Emission control versus concentration response; local improvement versus downwind transfer.
- Multi-pollutant interactions and climate penalties.
- Exposure, dose, baseline disease rate, and demographic vulnerability are distinct steps.
- Mechanism evidence may require source attribution, perturbation experiments, chemical regime diagnostics, or observations independent of the model.

## Energy and infrastructure

- Service demand, final energy, primary energy, emissions, water, reliability, and cost are not interchangeable outcomes.
- Capacity, dispatch, network constraints, storage, and extreme-hour adequacy can reverse annual-average conclusions.
- Embodied and operational impacts; direct and system-wide effects.
- Asset lifetime, technology turnover, stranded assets, and historical design assumptions.
- Rebound pathways through price, time, convenience, expectations, and induced demand.

## Cities and urban development

- Morphology, land use, transport, building stock, vegetation, heat, and pollution interact.
- City boundaries can hide imports, commuting, atmospheric transport, and supply-chain burdens.
- Separate place effects from population sorting and exposure mobility.
- Compare neighbourhood, city, metropolitan, and intercity scales when conclusions may aggregate differently.
- Planning recommendations need land, governance, affordability, and implementation constraints.

## Society, economics, and human behaviour

- State the behavioural mechanism: information, norms, salience, incentives, hassle, habits, substitution, or moral licensing.
- Distinguish preference, access, constraint, adoption, use, and persistence.
- Avoid interpreting model feature importance or mediation as causality without identification.
- Track incidence: who pays, who benefits, when, and under which baseline vulnerability.
- Test anticipation, displacement, spillovers, treatment heterogeneity, and general-equilibrium responses.

## Policy

- Define the instrument, target, enforcement, timing, jurisdiction, and compliance margin.
- Separate adoption from implementation and implementation from effect.
- Use appropriate causal designs where observational policy variation is central; test pre-trends, spillovers, concurrent policies, and treatment timing.
- Evaluate objective functions explicitly: emissions, welfare, health, reliability, equity, political feasibility, or resilience.
- Recommendations should describe the condition under which a policy works, not simply restate an association.

## Cross-domain chain

For coupled studies, draw the minimum chain:

`driver/intervention → physical or behavioural mechanism → system response → exposure/service change → welfare/distribution → decision`

Mark every link as measured, modelled, assumed, or outside scope. The weakest necessary link limits the final claim.
