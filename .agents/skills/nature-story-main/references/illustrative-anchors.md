# Illustrative story anchors

These papers illustrate transferable narrative moves in the requested domain. They are not a substitute for a live nearest-neighbour search and should not be cited as evidence for an unrelated project.

## Trade-off and policy-mix non-additivity

- Viguié, V. & Hallegatte, S. **Trade-offs and synergies in urban climate policies.** *Nature Climate Change* 2, 334–337 (2012). https://doi.org/10.1038/nclimate1434
  - Move: apparently undesirable stand-alone policies become viable in combinations because impacts are non-additive.
  - Transfer: test policy packages factorially instead of ranking instruments one at a time.

## Co-benefits that change the cost frame

- Vandyck, T. et al. **Air quality co-benefits for human health and agriculture counterbalance costs to meet Paris Agreement pledges.** *Nature Communications* 9, 4939 (2018). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-018-06885-9
  - Move: a local health/agriculture consequence changes the interpretation of global mitigation cost.
  - Transfer: connect engineering intervention to monetized or decision-relevant co-benefits without erasing regional heterogeneity.

## Assumption inversion through system reconfiguration

- Mercure, J.-F. et al. **Reframing incentives for climate policy action.** *Nature Energy* 6, 1133–1143 (2021). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-021-00934-2
  - Move: replaces the familiar free-rider framing with incentives produced by changing technology, trade, and stranded assets.
  - Transfer: identify when the accepted strategic frame fails after system boundaries or dynamics change.

## Temporal rebound and durable policy

- Wen, Z. et al. **Combined short-term and long-term emission controls improve air quality sustainably in China.** *Nature Communications* 15, 5169 (2024). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-49539-9
  - Move: short-term control produces rapid improvement followed by rebound; long-term structural policy changes the trajectory.
  - Transfer: align time scales and distinguish temporary suppression from durable system change.

## Mechanism-based sign reversal

- Liu, S. et al. **Divergent surface urban heat island responses to background aerosol mitigation.** *Nature Cities* 3, 654–664 (2026). https://doi.org/10.1038/s44284-026-00463-7
  - Move: the same air-quality intervention changes urban heat in opposite directions across hydroclimates, explained through radiative, rainfall, vegetation, and evaporative pathways.
  - Transfer: elevate heterogeneity only when an interaction mechanism predicts the sign change.

## Compound change and infrastructure consequence

- Perera, A. T. D. et al. **Challenges resulting from urban density and climate change for the EU energy transition.** *Nature Energy* 8, 397–412 (2023). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-023-01232-9
  - Move: climate change and urban densification jointly alter demand, cost, and reliability; the consequence appears at infrastructure planning scale.
  - Transfer: move from climate signal to system design and reliability under compound drivers.

## Distributional vulnerability and actionable prioritization

- Adshead, D. et al. **Climate threats to coastal infrastructure and sustainable development outcomes.** *Nature Climate Change* 14, 344–352 (2024). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41558-024-01950-2
  - Move: infrastructure disruption is translated into service-access and SDG consequences, revealing disproportionate threats and targeted adaptation leverage.
  - Transfer: couple hazard exposure to essential services and distribution rather than ending at asset risk.

## Planning under myopia and lock-in

- Heuberger, C. F. et al. **Impact of myopic decision-making and disruptive events in power systems planning.** *Nature Energy* 3, 634–640 (2018). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-018-0159-3
  - Move: a seemingly prudent wait-for-innovation strategy creates oversized or insufficiently decarbonized systems.
  - Transfer: compare sequential decisions with perfect-foresight planning and expose path dependence.

## Secondary writing resource

- **以一篇 Nature 为蓝本：五步打造高引用率科研故事线** (Zhihu, 2025). https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/1907454410130458610
  - Useful ideas: identify the core problem, use question–answer result sections, make each figure advance one story point, and align title/abstract with the central claim.
  - Caution: treat citation-rate and editorial claims in secondary writing advice as unverified unless independently sourced.
