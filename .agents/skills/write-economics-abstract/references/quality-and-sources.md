# Abstract Quality Gate and Source Map

## 1. Hard gates

The abstract cannot pass if it contains any of the following:

- an invented or unsupported core result, method, contribution, mechanism, sample, or implication;
- a wrong number, sign, unit, baseline, estimand, population, or period;
- association presented as causation;
- a suggestive or model-dependent channel presented as an established empirical mechanism;
- the paper's primary finding omitted or displaced by secondary material;
- an unresolved placeholder in formal output;
- a citation, table/section reference, or undefined abbreviation that breaks self-containment;
- output in the wrong formal language.
- a terminology substitution or qualitative label that changes the estimand, sample, design, or contribution.
- a final draft above 200 words without explicit user or outlet authorization;
- multiple paragraphs without an explicit format requirement;
- outer quotation marks, headings, code fences, block quotes, or other wrappers around the manuscript-ready prose;
- secondary accounting, calibration, decomposition, or implementation detail displacing the task, principal result/counterfactual, or essential boundary.
- a measured outcome or proxy silently broadened into a different economic object (for example, winning price into procurement cost).
- any paraphrase that upgrades causal status, mechanism strength, centrality, scope, welfare content, or contribution beyond the claim ledger.
- a constructed sample definition replaced by an inferred category label or stripped of its defining event, place/institution, threshold, or lookback window.
- a supportive, consistent-with, or suggestive mechanism sentence containing a causal predicate such as `drove`, `explains`, `operates through`, `mediates`, `because`, or `by reducing`, even if another word in the sentence hedges it.
- any repeated result or closing synthesis that drops the outcome's population, subgroup, proxy, place, horizon, or other scope-bearing qualifier.
- `modest`, `large`, `small`, `meaningful`, `important`, or another evaluative label without an explicit source benchmark or author evaluation.
- an assignment/randomization unit described as receiving an intervention delivered to a different recipient, or any other substitution among assignment unit, recipient, delivery actor, observation unit, and analysis unit.
- a candidate mechanism or latent friction substituted for the identified treatment as the subject of any effect, implication, or closing sentence.
- `reducing [candidate mechanism] can/does [outcome]`, `the findings indicate that [candidate mechanism] [effect]`, or equivalent mechanism-effect syntax when the channel is only supportive or consistent-with.
- a structural channel described as dominating, offsetting, outweighing, or stronger than another without an explicit source comparison, or an ambiguous theoretical net sign rewritten as a ranked mechanism story.

These failures cannot be compensated by style points.

## 2. 100-point rubric

Apply only after the hard gates pass.

| Dimension | Points | Full-credit standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Factual fidelity and traceability | 30 | Every substantive claim maps to the manuscript with correct scope and numbers |
| Genre function and information selection | 20 | Reader can recover task, answer method, main finding, and closure; secondary content is disciplined |
| Structure and logical flow | 15 | Functions appear in a natural, paper-specific order with clear dependencies |
| Economic and identification language | 10 | Causal, model, mechanism, magnitude, and welfare terms match evidence |
| Length and information density | 10 | Elastic target fits journal and complexity; every sentence earns space |
| Natural academic English | 10 | Precise, readable, non-promotional, non-template prose |
| Mode and delivery experience | 5 | Correctly drafts, rewrites, audits, or fact-checks with minimal unnecessary explanation |

A score above 95 requires no meaningful weakness in factual fidelity and no merely cosmetic compliance with the four functions.

## 3. Final checklist

- Does the opening establish a specific task, tension, object, or result?
- Does method language reveal credibility or interpretability rather than display technique?
- Are findings central, economically meaningful, and correctly quantified when appropriate?
- Is a null, contrast, or boundary included only when it changes the main claim?
- Is mechanism wording evidence-calibrated?
- Does the ending close the argument without forcing policy?
- Is the abstract self-contained and citation-free?
- Has every substantive sentence passed output-to-ledger and ledger-to-output review?
- Is the length justified rather than merely inside a range?
- Could a reader accurately explain the paper after reading only this paragraph?
- Is the delivered artifact one directly pasteable paragraph with no wrapper?
- Was the word count rechecked after a mandatory second compression pass?
- For structural work, is every accounting or decomposition detail indispensable to the main economic takeaway?
- Does every result noun name the exact measured or modeled object rather than a convenient umbrella term?

## 4. Normative and calibration layers

Keep two evidence layers distinct:

- **Guide-derived norm:** the original abstract guide supplies the normative purpose, four reader questions, credibility-centered method description, economically meaningful findings, direct closure, self-containment, exclusions, and compression discipline.
- **Sample calibration:** ten recent AER/QJE/JPE/Econometrica papers test and refine how these functions appear in practice. They show flexible order, paper-type variation, information-dense `We/This paper` openings, contrast-based result organization, and closure beyond policy.

Sample observations do not silently override the guide. A frequency in ten papers is not a universal Top Five rule.
