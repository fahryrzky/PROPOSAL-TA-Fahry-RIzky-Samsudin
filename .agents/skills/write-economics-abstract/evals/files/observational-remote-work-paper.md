# Working paper materials: Remote-Work Adoption and Firm Output

> **Note:** This is a synthetic teaching and testing packet. All names, data, results, and citations are fictional.

## Current body

The paper uses annual data for 320 business-service firms from 2017 through 2024. Firms chose when and whether to adopt a formal remote-work policy. A firm fixed-effects specification compares output per employee before and after adoption and includes year effects and observed workforce composition. Adoption is associated with a 6 percent increase in output per employee. Event-study estimates show that output was already rising at adopting firms in the two years before adoption, so the paper explicitly declines to interpret the estimate causally. Employment and wages also rise after adoption. Voluntary turnover falls by 1.2 percentage points, but this estimate is imprecise. A worker survey in 46 firms shows higher reported schedule control after adoption. The survey does not measure effort, monitoring, or office-cost savings.

The paper's preferred interpretation is descriptive: remote-work adoption occurs during periods of firm expansion and is associated with higher output, but selection and pre-trends prevent isolation of the policy's causal effect. The contribution is a new linked firm-worker panel, not a causal design.

## Existing abstract supplied by an earlier coauthor

“Using a novel natural experiment, we show that remote work raises productivity by 12 percent because firms save office costs. These results prove that governments should require firms to offer remote work.”

## Known limits

The sample consists of business-service firms with at least 50 employees. The paper has no office-cost data, no random or quasi-random policy variation, and no welfare analysis.
