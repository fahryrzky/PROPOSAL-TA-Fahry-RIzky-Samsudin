# Theory paper materials: AI Delegation and Worker Initiative

> **Note:** This is a synthetic teaching and testing packet. All names, data, results, and citations are fictional.

## Environment

The paper develops a two-period model in which a manager chooses how much decision authority to delegate to a worker. The worker can acquire costly task-specific information. An AI system supplies a public recommendation whose precision is known. The manager cannot contract on information acquisition, and the worker captures a share of project surplus through bargaining.

## Main propositions

When AI precision is low, more precise recommendations increase delegation because they reduce disagreement over routine decisions. Beyond a threshold, further increases in AI precision reduce delegation: the manager relies on the recommendation and the worker's return to acquiring private information falls. Worker information acquisition and total surplus are therefore hump-shaped in AI precision under the baseline parameter restrictions.

The non-monotonicity disappears when workers capture no surplus or when private information is costless. With sufficiently strong worker bargaining power, advanced AI can increase worker rents even as delegation falls. The paper also studies a complementary-AI case in which recommendations improve the value of worker information; delegation then rises monotonically.

## Scope

The paper is theoretical and contains no data, estimation, calibrated welfare calculation, or policy experiment. Its contribution is to distinguish substitutive and complementary AI and show the parameter conditions under which AI precision changes delegation, information acquisition, surplus, and rents.
